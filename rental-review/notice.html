NOTICE & FAIRNESS

A fair, documented process

A transparent process matters as much as the percentage. These are review steps—not a completed legal notice.

StepWhat to checkSource
1 · Confirm eligibilityReview lease and amendments, rent ledger, lawful base rent, last effective increase, coverage and current license. Do not infer a reset from a roommate replacement.Official source ↗
2 · Set a neutral policyUse the same documented criteria for each unit, including lawful eligibility, timing and a conservative affordability-sensitive choice. A lawful decision to increase one or both is fact-dependent; identical increases are not a safe harbor.Official source ↗
3 · Document the basisIdentify an annual allowable increase as the basis if that is what is used; clearly separate annual and banked portions. An ordinary annual increase does not require a petition; do not claim specific costs unless accurately supported.Official source ↗
4 · Written noticeFor periodic residential tenancies, California Civil Code §827 generally requires at least 30 days for increases of 10% or less and 90 days for more than 10%, considering other increases during the prior 12 months. Exceptions and longer terms may apply.Official source ↗
5 · ServiceThe Rent Board says add five days for mailed notice. Verify proper method, mailing location, deadlines and the lease with counsel; use personal delivery or mail as allowed by Civil Code §827. Keep proof.Official source ↗
6 · Clear detailsShow current lawful base, dollar and percentage increase, new total, effective date, annual/banked breakdown and the dates supporting any banked portion. Cite the basis and provide a contact for questions; do not use a generic template as legal approval.Official source ↗
7 · Avoid retaliationDo not raise rent with a dominant motive of retaliation for tenants exercising legal rights; never base differences on protected characteristics. Review both intent and effects.Official source ↗
8 · Review and respondConsider independent SF landlord counsel and invite tenants to consult an advocate; share calculations and correct errors. No attorney or advocate has actually approved this analysis.Official source ↗

One unit or both?

There is no verified requirement to raise both rents. Different eligibility dates or prior increases may justify different timing or amounts; the landlord must still use legitimate, consistently applied criteria and comply with fair housing and anti-retaliation rules. The same percentage for both does not cure an unlawful notice, an ineligible increase, discriminatory motive, or a disparate impact. Document the reason privately, without sensitive tenant data in public files.

What do large and small owners actually do?

No official verified dataset comparing the usual renewal practices of corporate and individual SF landlords was established. Their typical percentages and frequency are therefore left blank on the review page. An internal annual review with documented criteria is a suggested process, not a claim about industry norms.

Tenant support

The City lists tenant counseling for rent-increase notices and identifies free legal help for tenants who receive eviction court papers; eligibility for a free lawyer in every rent-increase dispute is not established. Rent Board counseling: 415-252-4600. City resource list ↗
