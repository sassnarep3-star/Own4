The process / One unit or both?

Raise one unit, or both? — and the tenant-lawyer review

Direct answer: raise both units under the same documented, formula-based policy. A one-unit-only increase is not automatically unlawful — but in a two-unit building owned by one landlord, it is the posture most likely to invite the inquiry the owner most wants to avoid. Below: the legal framework, the unit-specific wrinkles, and a scrutiny checklist.

Raise only Unit 1 (smaller increase exists for Unit 2? No — reversed)Not unlawful if based solely on the formula (anniversary date, cap, banking) and no protected-class motive or effect.Elevated — invites “why only us?” inquiry; burden of explanation falls on the owner.CA CRD — housing

Recommendation — implement both, uniformly:
  1. Adopt a written policy memo: “Both units receive the annual allowable increase on their anniversaries; banked amounts, where lawfully available and documented, are offered to each unit independently of who occupies it.”
  2. Serve the same style of notice on the same cycle each year (Unit 1 in Dec/Jan for a Feb 1 effective date; Unit 2 in Feb/Mar for a June 1 effective date).
  3. Unit 1: +1.6% ($63.92) in 2027 (optionally +1.4% banked with counsel sign-off). Unit 2: +10.1% unambiguous banked package (or annual-only) with 90-day notice in 2027 — supported by the 7-year paper trail, applied with the identical formula logic.
  4. Never reference occupants, family size, disability, vouchers, or length of residency as reasons in any document. Reference only: ordinance, published percentage, anniversary date, banked years, license.
  5. Have an owner-side attorney review the package; then (the brief’s standard) run it against the tenant-side checklist below; then serve.

If the reviewer still says “no”

Then the increase does not go out. The remedy ladder is: fix the defect → re-run the checklist → delay to the next anniversary if timing cannot be cured (a late increase banks again — the law rewards patience, not improvisation). For the owner’s own protection, consider written counsel review on file; for the brief’s standard (“pass review by a SF tenant lawyer”), share the same binder with tenant-side counsel through the SF Rent Board referral listing or have the tenant speak directly with the free counselor (415-252-4600) — transparency is the strongest antidote to allegations of abuse.
