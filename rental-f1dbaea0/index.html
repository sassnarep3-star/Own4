Rental increase research — 1940 rent-controlled building, SF 94122 (Outer Sunset)

A source-linked, line-by-line review of what a reasonable and normal rent increase would be for the two tenancies in this building — under San Francisco, California and federal law — and how to implement one so it survives scrutiny by a San Francisco tenant-side attorney. All tenant details are anonymized; dates and dollar amounts are preserved exactly as provided. No personal information is published anywhere in this project.

Read first — coverage must be confirmed, not assumed. The analysis below assumes the units are covered by the SF Rent Ordinance (building built 1940, before the 1979 cutoff, two apartments, not single-family homes or condominiums). Coverage turns on facts that are not in the brief: certificate-of-occupancy date, whether the units are condos, and whether the owner lives in one of the two units (which also drives the AB 1482 two-unit exemption). Bottom line

A reasonable and normal increase is the SF Rent Board annual allowable amount — 1.6% for increases effective 2026-03-01 through 2027-02-28 (0.016 × base rent). That is $63.92/month on Unit 1 and $50.40/month on Unit 2, applied once every 12 months with written notice: SF.gov Rent Board announcement (2026-01-14). Anything larger is only lawful through (a) correctly calculated banked increases for years the landlord skipped, or (b) a Rent Board–approved petition (capital improvement, operating & maintenance, etc.). Applying the same documented method to both units is the strongest answer to the discrimination question: raising only one unit is not automatically unlawful, but it is the option most likely to draw a tenant-side inquiry.

Recommendation in one line: confirm coverage with the Rent Board counselor → verify the rent-increase license is current → serve identical, formula-based written notices for both units (Unit 1: Feb 1, 2027; Unit 2: June 1, 2027 using the 2027-28 rate when published) → consider banked increases for Unit 2 only with counsel sign-off → document everything. 2. Under federal law?No federal rent cap exists. Federal law limits discrimination (Fair Housing Act) and collusion (antitrust — see RealPage), not amounts.Federal law · HUD — Fair Housing Act
3. Under state (California) law?AB 1482: 5% + CPI, max 10% (8.8% for SF, Aug 1 2026–Jul 31 2027), but a stricter local ordinance controls — so the 1.6% local cap governs. Just cause required; rent can’t exceed cap; 3× damages for willful excess.State law · Civ. Code §1947.12
