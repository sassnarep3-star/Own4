How owners actually handle increases — and the exact, lawful way to do it

What large operators do, what small owners typically do, the mandatory notice procedure, and the short list of legitimate reasons for an increase. Every legal step below links to an official source; market-practice characterizations are labeled as such and are not official statistics (flag F12).

2. How individual property owners handle rental increases in San Francisco

PracticeReality (labeled)Source
Take the annual allowable increaseMost common lawful posture: multiply base rent by the published rate (1.6% in 2026-27), once per 12 months, after written notice. This is what “normal” means operationally in SF.SF.gov — rent increases
Skip years and bankVery common among small owners — e.g., this building: no Unit 2 increase in 7+ years. Skipped increases bank (rules/reading disputed — flag F1) and remain usable for the life of the same tenancy.SF.gov — banked increases · flag F1
Rely on Rent Board counselors & formsSmall owners use the free counselor (415-252-4600) and standard forms rather than lawyers for routine annual increases; petitions are used only for qualifying work.SF.gov — counselor · SF.gov — petitions
Informal/Relational increasesSome owners raise below the cap to keep tenants — good will, but the cap arithmetic and notice duties still apply; verbal understandings are unenforceable against the cap. Practice characterization; not an official statistic.flag F12 · SF.gov — rent increases
Compliance failuresLate/no license, wrong rate from the wrong effective period, no banked-portion label, missing the once-per-12-months rule, mailed notices without +5 days — the Rent Board treats these as defects; tenants hold a free Form C remedy.SF.gov — rent increases · SF.gov — counselor

3. The correct way to inform tenants of a rent increase

3.1 Pre-conditions (before any notice)

  1. Confirm the units’ coverage & exemptions with the SF Rent Board counselor; document the CO date and condo/SFH status (flag F3).
  2. Pull the complete rent history per unit (dates, amounts, notices already served).
  3. File the annual Housing Inventory report (every March 1) and confirm the rent increase license is effective for the increase’s effective date; otherwise the increase is ineffective. SF.gov — Housing Inventory FAQ
  4. Check the tenant’s anniversary date and the once-per-12-months rule.
  5. Screen for Civ. Code §1942.5 exposure (any tenant complaint in prior 180 days) and any fair-housing exposure (protected-class timing). Civ. Code §1942.5

3.2 The written notice itself — required content

ElementRequirementSource
FormWritten; no special form required, but the Rent Board provides forms — keep a copy; serve per Civ. Code §827(b)(1).Civ. Code §827 · SF.gov — rent increases
Dollar amountExact dollar increase (e.g., $63.92 — never rounded up).SF.gov — rent increases
PercentageExact percentage (e.g., 1.6%), using the rate in effect on the increase’s effective date — not the rate in effect when served.SF.gov — rent increases
Effective dateMust be on/after the anniversary and ≥30 or ≥90 days after service; cannot be retroactive.Civ. Code §827
Banked portion identifiedIf any part is banked: state which part and for which years (e.g., “1.4% banked from 2025-26 + 1.6% 2026-27 annual”).SF.gov — banked increases
Rent Board fee allocation50/50 fee passthrough (annual fee) if being charged to tenant — separate from the increase; document if included. Fee amount UNVERIFIED (flag F8).flag F8 · SF.gov — Rent Board fee

3.3 Service

MethodRule (verified)Source
Personal deliveryHand to the tenant; note date/time/place; keep a copy and a service note.Civ. Code §827(b)(1)(A)
Mail (in-state)CCP 1013(a): in-state mail extends notice periods by 5 calendar days; identical extension applies to 30- and 90-day regimes ⇒ 35 / 95 days if mailed. Mail via USPS with postage paid, addressed to the tenant’s address as last given; attach/prepare certificate of mailing (unsigned copy notation is sufficient under CCP 1013(b)). SF Rent Board guidance agrees: “5 more days if mailed.”CCP §1013(a)–(b) · SF.gov — rent increases
Combined threshold rule≤10% alone or combined with any other increase in the prior 12 months ⇒ 30 days. >10% ⇒ 90 days. Count all increases in the window, not just this one.Civ. Code §827(b)(2)–(3)
Language / accessThe SF Rent Board publishes its materials in multiple languages (English, Spanish, Chinese, Filipino, Vietnamese); serving a translation alongside English is prudent and avoids any claim of ambush. Do not pressure, harass, or demand signatures of agreement — the notice operates regardless once properly served and effective.SF.gov — rent increases (multilingual docs)
Record retentionKeep: notice, proof of service, calculation worksheet, license evidence, payment ledger update, anniversary note (to avoid shifting anniversary dates). 7+ years recommended. Retention period is best practice, not a statutory citation.SF.gov — multiple-year increases (anniversary mechanics)

4. Reasonable (lawful) reasons for an increase — and the limits

Legitimate basisHow it works / limitsSource
CPI-based annual allowable (1.6% in 2026-27)60% of Bay Area CPI-U change; no justification letter required; formula is itself the reason.SF.gov — 2026-27 · SF.gov — rent increases
Banked prior-year increasesFor years you didn’t increase; add percentages, multiply current base rent, never compound; identify the banked part; ≥24-vs-12-month gate disputed (flag F1).SF.gov — banked increases
Capital improvement passthroughFile petition before notice; costs certified by Board; typically amortized over useful life; ≤10%/12-month combined with O&M; temporary displacement rules apply if work makes unit uninhabitable.SF.gov — petitions & passthroughs
Operating & maintenance expense increasePetition; for significant cost increases (e.g., taxes, insurance, maintenance) beyond normal; Board reviews documentation.SF.gov — petitions & passthroughs
Special circumstances / comparable unitsRare; comparable-unit rent evidence; Board approval.SF.gov — petitions & passthroughs
Utility passthroughs / GO-bond & water-bond worksheetsWorksheet (no full petition) or utility petition; must file worksheet before notice.SF.gov — petitions & passthroughs
Substantial rehabilitation exemptionBuilding 50+ years old, essentially uninhabitable, work ≥75% of new-construction cost; Board determination; building here is 86 years old (1940 → 2026) so the age prong is met if the rest is proven — do not use casually.SF.gov — substantial rehabilitation petitions
Costa-Hawkins / §6.14 partial vacancyOnly after last original occupant permanently leaves; notice rules for subsequent occupants; not available now in either unit.SF.gov — §6.14 / Costa-Hawkins
True vacancy — market resetNew tenancy ⇒ initial rent unregulated (no cap, no notice). The current units are occupied — not available.SF.gov — rent increases
Never legitimateRetaliation (§1942.5); discrimination (FHA/FEHA); roommate/occupancy surcharge; above-cap without approval; without license; without notice; retroactive; rounding up; harassment to induce vacancy (Admin. Code §37.10B harassment is unlawful).Civ. Code §1942.5 · CA CRD — housing · SF.gov — rent increases
