How rental increases are handled

Large real-estate corporations versus individual property owners in San Francisco. Practice patterns below are general market context (not official-source data) unless a source link is shown; legal limits are the same for both.

Comparison

AspectLarge real-estate corporations (institutional owners)Individual property owners (small landlords)
Decision process Centralized portfolio or regional policy; increases set by asset-management or revenue-management teams, often on a fixed annual calendar. Owner decides case-by-case, often informally and tied to personal finances or property costs.
Pricing method Often uses revenue-management software and market data to set increases at or near the legal maximum permitted by local rent control. More likely to apply smaller, occasional increases or skip increases entirely; less systematic.
Legal compliance Typically has in-house or outside counsel; maintains Rent Board licenses, Housing Inventory filings, and standardized notice templates. May be unaware of the rent increase license, Housing Inventory reporting, or exact notice content requirements.
Notice delivery Standardized written notices, often mailed with certified delivery and documented service dates. Often hand-delivered or emailed; service date and proof of notice may be poorly documented.
Consistency across units Applies the same percentage/policy across comparable units to limit discrimination exposure. More likely to treat tenants differently without documenting unit-specific reasons.
Response to tenant pushback Routes disputes through corporate channels or counsel; may refer to Rent Board processes. Direct negotiation with tenants; higher risk of informal or unlawful agreements.
Vacancy resets On vacancy (change in tenancy), may reset rent to market where permitted (Costa-Hawkins / SF Rent Ordinance), following corporate re-leasing standards. May reset to market on vacancy without realizing local restrictions or required registrations still apply.

Important: the same legal limits apply to both

Whether the owner is a corporation or an individual, both units here are covered by the San Francisco Rent Ordinance (building built 1940). The binding increase limit is the Rent Board's annual allowable increase (currently 1.6%), the notice rules (30-day / 90-day), the rent increase license requirement, and the Fair Housing Act anti-discrimination rules apply identically. The comparison above describes typical practice patterns; it is not verified against official sources for any specific company or owner.

What this means for your units

  • If you are an individual owner, treat compliance with the same rigor as a corporate owner: get/confirm the rent increase license, compute the allowable increase on base rent, serve compliant written notice, and apply the same process to both units.
  • If you manage through a corporation, confirm that its standard process includes SF Rent Board registration and Rent Board license compliance before serving any increase.
  • Document the business reason for every increase (annual allowable percentage, banked increase, or approved passthrough) so the file can withstand review by an SF tenant lawyer.
