California — Tenant Protection Act of 2019 (AB 1482) (state)

RuleWhat the law saysThese unitsOfficial source
Rent cap Annual increases limited to 5% + local CPI, or 10%, whichever is lower. CPI = change in CPI-U for All Items for the metro area (BLS). Not tighter than SF local control (1.6%); local Rent Ordinance governs SF.gov — AB 1482 report
San Francisco maximum 8.8% maximum increase effective August 1, 2026 through July 31, 2027 (5% + 3.8% CPI). Above the SF local 1.6% limit — not the binding cap SF.gov — AB 1482 report
Just cause eviction After all tenants have continuously and lawfully occupied for 12 months (or one tenant for 24 months), termination requires stated just cause; no-fault terminations require relocation assistance or rent waiver. SF's more protective just-cause eviction rules govern instead SF.gov — AB 1482 report • CA AB 1482 text
Effective / sunset Effective January 1, 2020; expires January 1, 2030. In effect as of 2026-11-06 SF.gov — AB 1482 report
Damages for overcharge Rent above the maximum is recoverable; up to three times the excess if willful/oppressive/fraudulent/malicious, plus attorney's fees. Deters exceeding the allowable increase SF.gov — AB 1482 report (Damages)

How to read this page

The binding, verifiable cap for both units is the San Francisco Rent Ordinance annual allowable increase (currently 1.6%). AB 1482's state cap (8.8% in SF) is higher and does not replace local control. Federal law adds anti-discrimination obligations but no rent cap. Amounts in the "computation" column are arithmetic on verified figures ($3,995 and $3,150 × 1.6%) and are labeled where they rely on unverified inputs (banked increases, post-2/28/27 percentage).
