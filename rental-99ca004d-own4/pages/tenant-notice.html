Correct way to inform tenants of a rent increase

Verified notice and timing rules for San Francisco rent-controlled units, plus reasonable business reasons and documentation steps. All legal limits are linked to official sources.

Before you serve any increase

StepRequirementOfficial source
1. Rent increase license Hold a rent increase license (obtained via Rent Board Housing Inventory reporting) before imposing any annual allowable or banked increase. SF.gov — Get a rent increase license
2. Confirm anniversary date One increase every 12 months; the first increase may be imposed 12 months after the lease began. Confirm each unit's anniversary date with the Rent Board. SF.gov — Timing and anniversary date
3. Confirm the percentage Use the allowable percentage in effect on the date of the increase, not the notice date (currently 1.6% effective 3/1/26–2/28/27). SF.gov — Rent increases
4. Compute on base rent Calculate on base rent only (includes housing services such as parking/storage); do not round up. SF.gov — Increase on base rent only

Notice content and timing

ItemRuleOfficial source
Written notice Must be in writing; personally delivered or served by mail under Code of Civil Procedure §1013. SF.gov — Giving notice • CA Civil Code §827(b)
Notice period — standard At least 30 days' written notice before the effective date. SF.gov — Giving notice • CA Civil Code §827(b)(2)
Notice period — large increase At least 90 days' written notice if the increase (alone or combined with another increase in the 12 months before the effective date) is more than 10% of the rent. SF.gov — Giving notice • CA Civil Code §827(b)(3)
Mailed notice Add 5 days if the notice is mailed. SF.gov — Giving notice
Required content State the dollar amount of the increase, the percentage amount, and the date the increase goes into effect. SF.gov — Giving notice
Frequency limit No more than one increase in any 12-month period. SF.gov — Timing and anniversary date

Reasonable business reasons for a rent increase

  • Annual allowable increase (recommended): the Rent Board's CPI-based percentage (currently 1.6%). This is the standard, verifiable "normal" increase for rent-controlled units.
  • Banked increases: allowable increases not taken in prior years, subject to 12-month eligibility rules (verify each unit's banked amounts with the Rent Board — flagged F2).
  • Capital-improvement passthrough: certain capital-improvement costs may be passed through via a Rent Board petition and approval — not by unilateral notice.
  • Operating cost increases: property taxes, insurance, utilities, and maintenance are legitimate context, but the increase itself must still stay within the annual allowable limit unless a Rent Board petition is approved.

Reasons that are NOT lawful grounds

Never base an increase on a tenant's protected characteristic (race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, disability under the Fair Housing Act; California adds further protected classes), on the tenant exercising a legal right (e.g., requesting repairs or filing a complaint — retaliation), or during a fixed lease term without a lease provision allowing it. Any increase above the allowable percentage requires Rent Board approval.

Recommended notice sequence

  1. Confirm rent increase license status and each unit's anniversary date (Rent Board counselor: 415-252-4600).
  2. Compute the increase on base rent at 1.6% (Unit 1: $63.92 → $4,058.92; Unit 2: $50.40 → $3,200.40), effective before 2/28/27 if using the current percentage.
  3. Draft written notice stating dollar amount, percentage, and effective date; allow at least 30 days (add 5 days if mailed).
  4. Serve the notice personally or by mail per CCP §1013 and document the service date.
  5. Have a San Francisco tenant-landlord attorney review the notice before service.
  6. Keep the served notice, license, and computation in the property file.
