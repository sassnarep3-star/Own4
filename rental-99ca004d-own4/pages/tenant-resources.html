Why attorney review matters for these units

  • San Francisco has strict tenant protections; tenants can obtain free tenant lawyer services and may challenge unlawful increases through Rent Board petitions or court.
  • An increase above the annual allowable percentage can expose the owner to the excess rent plus up to three times the excess if willful, plus attorney's fees (AB 1482 damages).
  • Any increase that targets a tenant because of a protected characteristic can violate the federal Fair Housing Act and California law.
  • Recommended practice: serve increases at the verified allowable percentage (1.6%), with a rent increase license, compliant 30-day written notice, and attorney review before service.

Practical checklist before serving an increase

  • Confirm rent increase license (Housing Inventory) is in place.
  • Confirm each unit's anniversary date with the Rent Board.
  • Compute on base rent at 1.6% (Unit 1: $63.92 → $4,058.92; Unit 2: $50.40 → $3,200.40).
  • Serve written notice (amount, percentage, effective date) at least 30 days before the effective date; add 5 days if mailed.
  • Have a San Francisco tenant-landlord attorney review the notices before service.
  • Keep served notices, computations, and license records in the property file.
