## Governing framework (all official sources)

1. **Local — San Francisco Rent Ordinance.** Units built on or before June 13, 1979 have rent control and eviction (just cause) protection. The building (1940) is covered, assuming it is not a statutorily exempt unit type. [SF.gov — Learn about San Francisco rental laws](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-san-francisco-rental-laws/)
2. **Local — Allowable increase.** Current allowable rent increase is **1.6%**, effective March 1, 2026 through February 28, 2027 (60% of the Bay Area CPI-U increase). Prior period 3/1/25–2/28/26: 1.4%. [SF.gov — Rent increases](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco/) [SF.gov — Annual increase announcement](https://www.sf.gov/news--annual-rent-increase-3126-22827-announced/)
3. **Local — Notice.** One increase every 12 months; first increase 12 months after the lease began; 30-day written notice (90 days if the increase, alone or combined in a year, exceeds 10%); +5 days if mailed; notice must state dollar amount, percentage, effective date; use the percentage in effect on the date of the increase. [SF.gov — Rent increases](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco/)
4. **Local — Rent increase license.** A landlord must hold a rent increase license (via Rent Board Housing Inventory reporting) before imposing annual allowable or banked increases. [SF.gov — Rent increases](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco/)
5. **State — AB 1482 (Tenant Protection Act of 2019).** Statewide cap of 5% + local CPI, maximum 10%; for San Francisco, 8.8% maximum effective August 1, 2026–July 31, 2027 (5% + 3.8% CPI). Units already covered by local rent control below 5% + CPI are exempt from the state rent cap; SF's more protective just-cause eviction rules govern instead. Effective 1/1/2020, sunsets 1/1/2030. [SF.gov — AB 1482](https://www.sf.gov/reports/august-2023/california-tenant-protection-act-2019-ab-1482) [CA Legislature AB 1482 text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=201920200AB1482)
6. **State — Civil Code 827.** 30-day written notice for increases of 10% or less (combined in the prior 12 months); 90-day notice if greater than 10%. [CA Civil Code §827](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=827.)
7. **Federal — Fair Housing Act.** Prohibits housing discrimination based on race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, disability. No federal rent-control statute exists; rent regulation is state/local. [HUD — Fair Housing Act overview](https://www.hud.gov/helping-americans/fair-housing-act-overview)

## Answers to your questions

**Reasonable/normal increase under local, state, federal law:** The SF Rent Ordinance's annual allowable increase (currently 1.6%) is the governing, verifiable "normal" increase for both units. State AB 1482 allows up to 8.8% in SF but does not apply a tighter cap where local control is lower. Federal law imposes no rent cap but prohibits discriminatory increases.

**Corporations vs. individual owners:** Large portfolios typically apply centralized, policy-driven, legally reviewed annual increases with rent increase licenses and uniform notice. Individual owners often apply increases less systematically — both are bound by the same Rent Ordinance limits, notice rules, and anti-discrimination law.

**Correct way to inform tenants:** Written notice stating the dollar amount, percentage, and effective date; 30 days in advance (90 days if the increase exceeds 10% combined in 12 months); +5 days if mailed; percentage must be the one in effect on the effective date; rent increase license must be held first. Reasonable reasons: annual CPI-based allowable increase, banked increases, documented operating-cost or capital-improvement passthroughs (via Rent Board petition where applicable). Increases must not be retaliatory or discriminatory.

**Tenant-lawyer scrutiny:** San Francisco tenants can obtain free or low-cost legal help (Eviction Defense Collaborative, Rent Board counselor, Rent Board Referral Listing). An increase computed at the annual allowable percentage, with proper license and notice, applied uniformly, is the approach most likely to withstand review.

**One unit or both units?** Raising rent on one unit only is lawful if driven by unit-specific, non-protected factors (different anniversary dates, base rents, tenancy start dates, banked increases). To minimize discrimination exposure, apply the same documented annual allowable increase process to both units and treat similarly situated tenants consistently. Never base an increase on a protected characteristic.
