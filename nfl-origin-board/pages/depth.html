Depth & deficiency

The 2nd/3rd/4th-string deficiency layer, and exactly why its columns are empty.

This is the part of the brief that tracks 2nd, 3rd and 4th string players replacing starters on both sides of the ball. It is also the part that is most completely blocked, and the reason is a single missing source rather than missing effort.

Why nothing here is filled in. The injury report tells you who is on it. It does not tell you who replaces them, or whether that replacement is a 2nd or a 4th string player. That needs a depth chart or a snap count. The brief rejects jersey order, experience and salary as proxies (R-50), and R-52 requires the depth-order source to be resolved for all 32 clubs before the feature is built. Neither route has been verified, so every depth value on this site is blank.

What is not being done, by rule

  • R-50. Rejected: deriving depth order from jersey number, experience or salary.
  • R-11. Depth must be modelled from playing time, not from jersey order.
  • R-33. Depth slots are groups, not a single starter. Several clubs list more than one player at rank 1 in a slot; treating column 1 as the starter would fabricate a starter the club never named.
  • R-51. Shared and empty slots must be represented faithfully, not flattened to a ranked list.
  • R-52. Resolve the depth-order source before building the backup-deficiency feature. Route (a) is per-club official depth pages, checked club by club; route (b) is snap counts. Neither is verified.
  • R-53. Score injury-designation reliability before using the designation as a feature.

What you can see today instead

The per-team view publishes the official count of Out, Questionable and Doubtful players per club, and names replacements only where a dated official nfl.com article does. That is 28 clubs with counts and 12 clubs with a named starter statement. It is not a deficiency matrix, and it is not presented as one.
