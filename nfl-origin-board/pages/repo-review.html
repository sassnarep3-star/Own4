The finding that matters

Ten pages of this project publish data rows with no source link at all. The brief requires a source link on every table row. Some of these rows are internal decisions that have no official source by nature — requirement registers, question lists, verdicts — which is precisely why a convention is needed rather than a rule that is quietly broken. Conflicting captures of the same official page

The nfl-lines workspace captured the Week 10 injury report on 2026-11-12 and recorded 275 player rows. This session captured the same page on 2026-11-16 and found 347. The per-club counts differ too: New England shows 15 listed with 0 Questionable there, and 15 listed with 12 Questionable here. Neither is wrong — the report grows and designations appear through the week. But no page states the capture day inside the number itself, which is why two different counts for the same page now circulate in one repository (RF-12).

A completed backtest exists, before the price policy was decided

nfl-lines publishes a finished backtest of 15 strategies covering 19,264 bets, with win rate, ROI, maximum drawdown, prices and p-values, each split into a 2011-2021 development period and a later confirmation period. Overall ROI is positive for 5 of 15 strategies; in the confirmation period, 3 of 15 are positive. The prices come from the nflverse archive via nfl-lines/data/raw, not from a licensed feed. R-20 says decide the price-data policy before publishing any backtest, and R-09 forbids backtest numbers on a leaderboard. This session publishes no backtest, no ROI and no price. The question for the owner is whether that backtest is intended to stand, and under which policy (RF-14).
