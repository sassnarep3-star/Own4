Leaderboard

Signal and staking scored separately, so a lucky sizing run cannot mask a bad prediction.
Two scores, deliberately disjoint. The signal score is computed from prediction quality only — how close our number lands versus the market's, accuracy against the break-even rate, and the evidence against luck. No stake appears anywhere in it. The staking score is computed from the sizing rule: flat ROI, quarter-Kelly ROI, the difference between them, and drawdown. A strategy can rank highly on one and badly on the other, and the leaderboard shows both so the difference is visible.

Line skill is market MAE minus our MAE, in points, from the line-skill measurement — it is the same number for every strategy, because it measures the model, not the bet selection. Accuracy gap is hit rate minus the break-even rate implied by the prices actually taken. Luck p is the price-implied null described on the gates page. Staking α is Kelly ROI minus flat ROI: positive sizing added value, negative means it destroyed it.

Why the split matters — the S7 case

S7 is the best strategy in this house on ROI: +56.1% flat across 7 bets. Its staking score (61.0) is higher than its signal score (53.0). On a single-column leaderboard sorted by return, S7 would sit at the top and look like the strongest idea on the board. Separated, it reads correctly: 7 parlays, a luck-test p of 0.2194, and demotion trigger D3 already firing. This is precisely the failure the split exists to prevent, and it appeared in the first week we ran it.
