Questions & answers

Before any decision can reasonably be made: what we know, what we do not, and how to find out.
What we know, what is missing, and what would settle it — 18 questions. The point of this register is to separate the things that are unknown because nobody has looked from the things that are unknown because no source exists. Those need different responses and it is expensive to confuse them.
Q-01 · Where do the market numbers come from, and are they official? open Prices
What we know
The NFL publishes no odds on nfl.com. Every market number in this workspace comes from the nflverse/nfldata archive, which is a third-party CC BY 4.0 repository of historical closing lines. It is reconciled here against official scores (136/136 exact), so the results line up, but the prices themselves carry no official provenance.
What is missing
An official or licensed price feed. Without one, no published edge, ROI or closing-line value can be described as verified.
What would settle it
Licence a price feed (Sportradar or Genius are the NFL's authorised distributors) or buy a commercial odds archive and re-run build.py against it. Nothing else changes.
Source
https://github.com/nflverse/nfldata
Q-02 · Can we record opening numbers, or only closing numbers? open Prices
What we know
No opening number exists for any 2026 market in any source available to this repository. The nflverse archive files were enumerated directly: closing_lines.csv ends at 2018, sc_lines.csv ends at 2020, and initial_lines.csv covers 2021 only. That is 0 of 900 price rows with an opening number.
What is missing
The entire opening side of the price-snapshot store for 2026.
What would settle it
A feed that captures a timestamped price the moment a market is posted. Until then the opening column stays blank on every row and the store is half-built, which is exactly how it is published.
Source
https://api.github.com/repos/nflverse/nfldata/contents/data
Q-03 · Is the market number we compare against a closing number or something else? open Prices
What we know
The archive snapshot carries one price per market per game, captured at commit 6ea0be6 (2026-11-12). It is treated as the closing number. The capture time is not published by the archive, so "closing" is an assumption about the archive, not a verified timestamp.
What is missing
A verified capture timestamp, and therefore any guarantee that the number was available before kickoff.
What would settle it
Capture prices ourselves at a known time, or obtain a feed that publishes its capture time.
Source
https://github.com/nflverse/nfldata
Q-04 · How do we know whether a replacement is a 2nd, 3rd or 4th string player? open Depth
What we know
The NFL publishes no depth chart. The team depth-chart route on nfl.com returns 404 (re-tested this session). The official injury report gives player, position, injury, practice status and game status — and nothing about depth order.
What is missing
The depth ordinal the brief asks for. It is blank on every row.
What would settle it
Two official routes exist and neither is used yet: (1) the official gamebook PDF for each game publishes that game's starting lineups, so a player who started in week w-1 and is Out in week w has a replacement who can be named from the week-w gamebook; (2) official per-game participation gives an ordinal count of prior starts. Licensing a depth chart is the fast route and is suggestion S-03.
Source
https://www.nfl.com/teams/new-england-patriots/depth-chart
Q-05 · Which players are actually starting for each team in Week 10? partially answered Depth
What we know
Four replacements are named in official NFL.com news reports this week: Drew Lock starts at QB for Seattle, Deshaun Watson for Cleveland, Carson Beck for Arizona, and Kyle McCord is in line for his first start at QB for Miami. Baltimore has lost two quarterbacks (Lamar Jackson, 6-10 weeks; Tyler Huntley, out for the season) and the official reports read this session do not name their replacement.

Q-06 · Do our originated numbers beat the market numbers? answered Model
What we know
No. On 105 walk-forward graded games our originated spread misses the official margin by 9.383 points on average against the market's 8.814 (we are 0.569 worse, paired p = 0.138), and our total misses by 11.112 against the market's 10.357 (we are 0.755 worse, paired p = 0.046). The total gap is statistically significant in the wrong direction.
What is missing
A model that beats the market. There is not one in this workspace.
What would settle it
This is settled as a negative result for the current model. The next step is the modelling work in suggestion S-05, not more data.
Source
https://www.nfl.com/schedules/2026/by-week/week-1-10
Q-07 · Is the model leaking future information? answered Model
What we know
Structurally no. A number originated for week w uses only official games with week < w plus the official 2025 final standings, which were published before the 2026 season began. The pipeline has no path by which a later game can enter an earlier one.
What is missing
An independent adversarial check of the walk-forward boundary.
What would settle it
Re-run build.py with week w+1 deleted from the input and confirm the week-w numbers are byte-identical.
Source
https://www.nfl.com/standings/league/2025/REG
Q-08 · Why do home teams cover only 40.9% of the time in 2026? open Market behaviour
What we know
Over 137 official results the mean market spread is +1.69 points to the home team while the mean official home margin is -0.02. Home sides therefore fail to cover far more often than half the time: 56 covers, 75 losses, 6 pushes, a 40.9% cover rate. The sign convention was verified three independent ways, so this is not a parsing error.
What is missing
Whether this is a durable 2026 effect or one season of noise about a true 50% rate (z is about -2.1, so it is around the edge of conventional significance on its own).
What would settle it
Compute the same figure for 2019-2025 from an archived line source. If the home-cover rate is stable near 41% across seasons it is an exploitable structural effect; if it wanders around 50% it is noise.
Source
https://www.nfl.com/schedules/2026/by-week/week-1-10
Q-09 · Can any strategy qualify for the leaderboard yet? answered Validation
What we know
No. The pre-registered sample floor is 300 graded bets and the season has produced 137 official results, so the largest bet count any strategy reaches is 77. Separately, after Benjamini-Hochberg correction across the eight-strategy family, the smallest q-value is 0.165 — nothing is close to the 0.05 threshold.
What is missing
Sample. Nothing else is blocking qualification.
What would settle it
Time, plus continuing to grade. Week 11 alone adds about 14 games.
Source
https://www.nfl.com/schedules/2026/by-week/week-1-10
Q-10 · How do we keep a lucky staking run from looking like a good signal? answered Validation
What we know
By scoring them separately and publishing both. The clearest example this week is S7, the two-leg cross-game parlay: 7 bets, a +56.1% flat ROI, and a staking score of 61.0 that is HIGHER than its signal score of 53.0. On ROI alone S7 is the best strategy in the house. With the two scores separated it is visibly a 7-bet sample.
What is missing
Nothing. This is working as designed.
What would settle it
n/a — the gate D3 (luck-test p above 0.20 sends a live strategy back to paper) already fires on S7 at p = 0.219.
Source
https://www.nfl.com/schedules/2026/by-week/week-1-10
Q-11 · Can player-prop combinations be originated at all? open Props
What we know
No. Player props need a posted prop price, and neither the NFL nor any other official source publishes one. The official player pages do publish per-game logs, so outcomes are knowable; prices are not.
What is missing
Every prop price. The strategy registered for props, S8, has zero bets because no price exists to originate against.
What would settle it
Licence a prop price feed. The outcome side is already covered by the official per-game logs.
Source
https://www.nfl.com/players/drake-maye/stats
Q-12 · Can we build point-in-time team statistics from official sources? answered Data
What we know
Not from the team-stats pages. Those accept only the season-to-date selection; the weekly variant returns 404 (re-tested this session). A season-to-date table read today contains games that had not happened at the time of any earlier week, so using it in a backtest would be look-ahead bias.
What is missing
Official weekly splits for team statistics.
What would settle it
Two options: sum the official per-game box scores (point-in-time by construction, but one fetch per game), or reconstruct the splits from a licensed feed. This workspace avoids the problem by using only official final scores plus the prior-season standings as features.
Source
https://www.nfl.com/stats/team-stats/offense/passing/2026/reg/9
Q-13 · Is the third-party archive complete and current? answered Data
What we know
Almost, but not quite. All 137 games played through Thursday 12 November have a market line in the archive, but the archive carries no score for the Week 10 WAS-NYG game — its snapshot predates that game. Official results and archive scores agree exactly on the other 136 games.
What is missing
One game of settlement in the archive.
What would settle it
Nothing to fix: this workspace takes results from nfl.com and prices from the archive, so the gap does not propagate. It is recorded so that nobody assumes the two files agree row for row.
Source
https://www.nfl.com/games/commanders-at-giants-2026-reg-10
