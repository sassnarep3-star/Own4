What the gates are

GateRequirementWhyOfficial input
G1At least 300 graded betsA hit-rate claim on a smaller sample than this is not distinguishable from a run.nfl.com ↗
G2Luck test p ≤ 0.05Simulated null: every bet wins with the probability its own price implies, so the null ROI is centred on zero by construction. Permuting the observed win/loss labels instead would preserve the observed hit rate and always return p ≈ 0.5, so it is not used.nfl.com ↗
G3Bootstrap 95% CI lower bound on flat ROI above zeroPoint estimates at n = 50 are noise; the interval has to clear zero.nfl.com ↗
G4Binomial test against the break-even rate implied by the prices taken, p ≤ 0.05The break-even rate is computed from the actual prices on each bet, not assumed to be 52.38%.nfl.com ↗
G5No look-aheadStructural: a number for week w uses only official games with week < w plus the pre-season prior.nfl.com ↗
G6Both halves of the sample profitableCatches a strategy that made all of its money in one stretch.nfl.com ↗
G7Benjamini-Hochberg q ≤ 0.05 across the eight-strategy familyTesting eight ideas at p < 0.05 would produce a false positive roughly a third of the time without this.nfl.com ↗
