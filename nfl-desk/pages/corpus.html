How many games are settled, and why nobody agrees

Four numbers describe the same 2026 season: 136 in the brief, 137 in two feeds, 150 in one transcription. The official standings give a ceiling. Until the feeds are reconciled week by week, this page publishes all four and settles none.

The implied count is a ceiling: it assumes every counted team-game was one half of a normal two-club game. It is consistent with 150 and inconsistent with 136, but it cannot distinguish 150 from 149 if one game were counted for only one club.

What the "136" most plausibly is. The only file in the repository with exactly 136 rows is the originated-line file, nfl-lines/data/derived/own_lines_week10.json (136 rows, weeks 10-18). It is a forward schedule with a fair line on every fixture and a market value on only 27 of them; 109 fixtures have no market number at all. So 136 is a count of fixtures with a fair line, not of settled games — which is why it cannot be compared with the 137 and 150 settled counts. → the file

What this does not prove. 150 is a ceiling derived from the standings, not a verified fixture list. It rules out a settled total of 136 only if every completed game appears in both clubs' records — which the zero remainder supports but does not certify.

4. Why the corpus matters more than the model

Every gate in this repository is a function of n. At the effect sizes actually observed, 80% power needs 1,148-1,290 bets per strategy, and the largest current sample is 303. A settled-game corpus that is 13 games shorter than believed moves every one of those numbers. That is why the corpus is settled before more modelling is added, not after.
