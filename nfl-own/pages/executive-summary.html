Executive summary

Snapshot 2026-11-13 (UTC). Plain-language version: the data is solid, the model is honest, and nothing has earned the right to be called a pick.

One paragraph. We re-read the official NFL.com standings, four team-stat tables, the Week 11 schedule, the official injury report and seven per-game player logs on 2026-11-13. Every publishable number reconciles: 32 clubs, W = L, PF = PA, pass percentage = completions over attempts, and seven player game logs sum to their published season totals. From that we originate Week 11 lines and grade nineteen strategies over a walk-forward holdout of 2,519 decisions. Zero strategies pass all twelve gates. The own-line model loses to the closing line on both spread (MAE 10.752 vs 9.850) and total (11.113 vs 10.477), so it is a structured projection rather than a price. Player props are gradeable for the first time in this repository, but the sample is seven players. Nothing here is wagering advice.

Three real choices

  • Do not publish picks or prices. The evidence does not support it and the market data does not exist.
  • Keep the leaderboard as a rejection machine. It rejected everything, which is the correct output for public data against efficient prices. Its value is stopping a lucky backtest from becoming a paid strategy.
  • Buy data or change the question. Two requirements are blocked by licensing, not by method: per-game player data is free and official, but a depth chart and a price archive are not. Suggestions S-02, S-03 and S-04 are the decision.
