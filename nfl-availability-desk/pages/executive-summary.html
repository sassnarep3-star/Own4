The five findings that change how the project should be run

  1. The Week 10 report is 347 rows across 28 clubs, not the 254 or 279 the brief quotes. 6 dated captures of the same official page sit in this repository and disagree (326, 326, 107, 347, 276, plus this capture at 347). 5 are strict subsets: the failure mode here is coverage, not accuracy. Designation coverage is 171/347 rows (49.3%).
  2. 0 clubs publish a section with no injury label — the brief's 'four no-injury sections' and 'Seattle and Cincinnati injury-free' statements do not reproduce; the 4 missing clubs are the official bye block (CHI, DEN, PHI, TB), reconciled by the build.
  3. The three Lions designations (Joseph, Mays, Reed) are published as Out, with the injury names and DNP practice status — the fix the brief asked for is already in the live data, and this capture proves it row by row.
  4. Two hard official contradictions, both decision-grade: a traded player (Henley) is still on his former club's report five days after the trade was announced; and Nacua's report row leaves the injury cell blank while dated text says IR with three named injuries. The report cannot be trusted as durable or as a severity source without a transaction join.
  5. The corpus number in the brief does not reproduce, and the right number is derivable: 145 games credited in the official standings now, 133 through Week 9, 147 when Week 10 finishes; the standings balance exactly (sum PF 6664 = sum PA 6664), which is what makes a results feed gradeable at all.

What is deliberately not on this site, and why

  • No price, no residual, no ROI, no CLV, no rewards paid. Not a gap in the effort — the league publishes none, and 24 sibling workspaces of blanks prove it. DEC-P1 is the owner's to make.
  • No starter/backup rank and no snap counts. The report publishes neither; the brief forbids inferring them from jersey order, salary or an image read by eye.
  • No win-rate or ROI leaderboard. Rankings require a proper score with an interval and a sample floor of n ≥ 300, so the board is empty by rule.
  • No ablation result. The with/without-personnel-features comparison is specified and frozen, but one dated capture cannot support it.
  • No 136-game claim. The count is published as measured, with the discrepancy named.

If a reviewer does three things

  1. Answer DEC-P1 (price policy, SUG-01) — it decides six blocked requirement rows and is the only blocker for the whole market side of the project.
  2. Approve the scheduled collector (SUG-02 A) — the code half is done (capture + drift alarm); the schedule half is one workflow, and it converts every single-week limitation into a series: deltas, ablations, designation reliability, point-in-time replay.
  3. Approve the game-book parser (SUG-03 A) — one official PDF per fixture already exists and is linked here; parsing it is what makes 'who actually started' a dataset instead of a suspicion.
