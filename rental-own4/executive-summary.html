2. What the law allows right now

  • SF allows 1.6% once every 12 months (3/1/26–2/28/27, set 2026-01-14 as 60% of Bay Area CPI-U change) on base rent only, no rounding up. (S2 ↗ S1 ↗)
  • Banking: Unit 2 may lawfully reach 13.1% (11.5% banked + 1.6%) = $412.65 → $3,562.65, but needs a 90-day notice, an itemized bank disclosure, the rent increase license, and a complete rent history — and a tenant may petition to challenge it. (S4 ↗ S3 ↗)
  • Unit 1 cannot touch its skipped Feb 2026 1.4% yet — a skipped increase banks only after 24 full months without any increase. (S4 ↗)
  • California adds process only: 30/90-day written notice (Civ. Code §827), anti-retaliation (§1942.5), and AB 1482’s looser ceiling (5%+CPI, max 10%) is preempted in effect by the stricter local cap. Federal law adds nothing on amount; it polices discrimination (FHA §3604). (S8 ↗ S10 ↗ S13 ↗)

3. Recommendation

Raise both units by 1.6%, same effective date, same 30-day notice process, after filing the Housing Inventory / rent increase license. Unit 1: $3,995.00 → $4,058.92 (+$63.92). Unit 2: $3,150.00 → $3,200.40 (+$50.40). Keep the Feb 1 and June 1 anniversaries, or shift deliberately and knowingly. Hold the 13.1% Unit 2 bank in reserve — lawful only with perfect documentation; expect a petition; the pending (not enacted) banked-increase cap bill would compress it further.

Do not raise only one unit. Not unlawful per se, but a single-household 1.6% increase invites disparate-treatment questions from tenant counsel/CRD with zero upside. Uniform formula + uniform date + identical notices = normal business practice, defensible before the Rent Board, CRD, or a court.
