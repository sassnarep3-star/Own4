What is a reasonable, normal increase for rent-controlled SF units?The SF Rent Board allowable annual increase: 1.4% (3/1/25–2/28/26) and 1.6% (3/1/26–2/28/27), calculated as 60% of Bay Area CPI-U change. Increases may be imposed once every 12 months.S1 ↗ S2 ↗
Does AB 1482 change this?No — AB 1482 (5% + CPI, max 10%) is a floor for covered units statewide; the stricter local Rent Ordinance governs these fully-covered units.S10 ↗ S5 ↗
Is there a federal cap?No federal rent control exists. Federal law (Fair Housing Act) governs discrimination, not amount.S13 ↗

What if a tenant moves out?Costa-Hawkins vacancy decontrol lets the owner reset the rent to market on a voluntary vacancy (continuing tenancy blocks this mid-stream). Unit 1 has a continuing occupant; no reset applies.S9 ↗

Recommended path

  1. Both units, 1.6%, same effective date. Unit 1: $3,995.00 → $4,058.92 (+$63.92/mo). Unit 2: $3,150.00 → $3,200.40 (+$50.40/mo). Annual, CPI-formula, zero discrimination surface.
  2. Serve 30-day written notices (or 90-day if you ever exceed 10% in any 12-month window), personal delivery or mail (+5 days), stating dollar amount, percentage, effective date. If any portion were banked, the notice must itemize it by year.
  3. File/report in the Rent Board Housing Inventory and obtain the rent increase license before imposing the increases (required since July 1, 2022; March 1, 2023 for buildings with 1–9 units).
  4. Keep the 13.1% Unit 2 banking option in reserve — lawful in principle, but only with a complete rent history, itemized notice, and willingness to defend a tenant petition. Not recommended this cycle.
  5. Do not raise only one unit. If business considerations require different treatment, document a written, formula-based rationale and get counsel sign-off first.
