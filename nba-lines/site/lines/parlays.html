Parlays and same-game combinations

Joint pricing with measured dependence instead of the independence shortcut, plus three graded parlay constructs.

Parlay arithmetic that matters. A two-leg parlay's fair price is the joint probability of both legs, and the joint is not the product unless the legs are independent. For legs in different games independence is an assumption (flag F09). For legs in the same game it is false, and the size of the error is measurable.

Graded parlay strategies

Each construct is graded on how well its joint probability predicted the realised joint outcome, across every graded game. The baseline is the unconditional realised joint rate — a model that cannot beat the base rate has added nothing.

The dependence adjustment is real but small for team markets. With r = +0.035, switching from independence to the measured joint moves the Brier score by +0.0003 at most. Pricing a spread-plus-total SGP as independent is therefore a rounding error, not a business model. Player-level dependence is a different story — see the props page, where two-leg combos move by up to 0.05 in probability.
Cross-game parlays get worse, not better. S06's joint Brier is higher than the base rate (skill -0.0241): chaining two unrelated legs compounds error faster than it compounds edge, and no cross-game dependence was verified to exploit.

Rule of construction

fair_decimal = 1 / P(both legs)
P(both)       = P(leg1) * P(leg2)                     only if independent
P(both)       = bivariate_normal(mu1, s1, s2, rho)    what this page uses
fair_american = -100p/(1-p) if p >= 0.5 else +100(1-p)/p
