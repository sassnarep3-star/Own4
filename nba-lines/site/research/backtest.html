The luck test, in plain terms

Shuffling the pairing between a prediction and the game it was made for destroys any real forecasting relationship while keeping both distributions intact. Repeat 4,000 times and you get the range of skill scores that pure chance produces with these exact predictions. The observed score is then just a percentile in that distribution.

The strongest result, S04, sits at p = 0.0002: fewer than 1 shuffle in 3,000 matched it. That is genuine evidence of forecasting information in team ratings — the least surprising true thing in sports analytics. It is not evidence of a betting edge, because skill at predicting outcomes is not the same as skill at beating a price.

Method

skill        = 1 - metric_model / metric_baseline       baseline fitted on pre-tip history only
bootstrap    = paired resample of games, 4,000 draws, percentile interval
permutation  = shuffle prediction-to-outcome pairing, 4,000 draws, (k+1)/(B+1)
ECE          = sum_b |observed_b - predicted_b| * n_b / n          5 equal-count bins
seed         = 20261105

Methods: scikit-learn time-series validation, calibration, NIST. The whole run is seeded and byte-reproducible.
