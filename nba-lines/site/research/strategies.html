Families and what distinguishes them

Singles

S01–S05 all price off the same rating backbone but differ in the signal layered on top: none (S01, S02), an independent totals model (S03), rest (S04) or recent form (S05). Because they share a backbone, their results are correlated, which is why the leaderboard's separation between them is not treated as meaningful. The honest reading is that the backbone carries the signal and the overlays are indistinguishable on 96 games.

Parlays

S06–S08 exist to test a specific claim: that dependence matters. They are graded on joint-probability accuracy, not on a bet record, because a bet record would require market prices that do not exist here.

Player props

S09–S12 are the strategy class most people actually want. They need three things this repository does not have from an official source: per-player game logs at usable volume, minutes projections, and an official pace/defence table. Each is specified; each is blocked; all four are flagged.

Why no strategy is called a winner. A positive skill score is not a validated strategy. The gate list requires sample size, a bootstrap interval excluding zero, a permutation p below 0.01, calibration within tolerance, multiple-testing control and a price archive. Three of those fail, so the correct verdict is 'promising backbone, unvalidated strategy'.
