What each gate is for

G3 — sample sufficiency

96 scored games against a 300-game requirement. At a true skill difference of 0.05 in Brier score, 96 games cannot distinguish a good model from a lucky one; the standard error of the skill estimate alone is around 0.07. This single gate is why the leaderboard pays nobody.

G4 — out-of-sample positive skill

A paired bootstrap over games, not a single point estimate. The interval must exclude zero. S01, S04 and S05 clear it; S02 and S03 do not. The bootstrap's percentiles are computed from 4,000 resamples of the graded set.

G5 — luck test

The observed skill is compared with the distribution of skill obtained when the prediction-to-outcome pairing is shuffled 4,000 times. This is the closest thing to a direct measurement of 'how often would noise look this good'.

G6 — calibration

Five equal-count bins, expected calibration error, and a reliability slope. A model can rank games correctly and still be badly calibrated, and a mis-calibrated probability is unusable for pricing regardless of its discrimination. This gate fails: ECE 0.104.

G9 — market price evidence

The hard one. Recognising an edge requires a price someone else will take the other side of. Without a price archive there is no edge to measure, only a model opinion. No official price source exists in this repository, so this gate cannot pass, no matter how good the model gets.

Method sources: scikit-learn cross-validation, probability calibration, NIST hypothesis-testing handbook.
