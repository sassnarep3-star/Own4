Reading these numbers

Margin versus total: the null result that saves money

r = +0.0180 (95% CI -0.161 to +0.196). Margin and total are effectively independent within a game over these 121 finals. Practical consequence: a spread-plus-total same-game parlay can be priced by multiplication with negligible error, and any model that claims a large dependence benefit from that pair is wrong.

Player stats: the dependence that does matter

Within-player residuals correlate at roughly 0.15–0.27 across the three scoring stats. Because these are demeaned per player, the coefficients are not contaminated by the fact that good players are good at everything; they measure whether a player's deviation in one stat travels with his deviation in another in the same game. It does, mildly — minutes and game script move all three together.

Teammates: the one that does not clear the bar

The teammate points correlation is negative, which is what a usage trade-off should look like, but p = 0.060 and q = 0.075. It is not significant, so the teammate-stack strategy (S11) stays ungraded and no stack line is published (flag F08).

Cross-game: an assumption, labelled as one

Consecutive same-day totals correlate at a level that neither confirms nor rejects independence. The cross-game parlay therefore uses independence, and flag F09 records that this is an assumption rather than a verified fact.

Method

r        = Pearson product-moment correlation
95% CI   = Fisher z transform: tanh(atanh(r) +/- 1.96/sqrt(n-3))
p        = two-sided Student t with df = n - 2
q        = Benjamini-Hochberg step-up across the whole test family

Method sources: Benjamini & Hochberg (1995), NIST/SEMATECH handbook.
