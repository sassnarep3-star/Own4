The rules, fixed before the first result

  1. Entry. A strategy is a named, fully specified rule committed before it is graded. Changing a parameter creates a new entry and the old one stays on the board.
  2. Grading. Only out-of-sample predictions count. A strategy is graded on the metric appropriate to it: Brier for probabilities, MAE for point estimates, joint Brier for parlays.
  3. Eligibility. All ten gates must pass simultaneously: n ≥ 300, bootstrap lower bound > 0, permutation p < 0.01, calibration in tolerance, multiple-testing control, and a price archive for G9.
  4. Rewards. 40/25/15 points for ranks one to three. No money is funded, and a strategy that fails a gate forfeits its rank to nobody rather than to the next entry — dilution on failure would reward the absence of scrutiny.
  5. Correctness beats novelty. A strategy that reproduces a known result, honestly measured, outranks one that reports a larger number it cannot reproduce.

Why the ranking is not the story

S04, S05 and S01 finish within 0.005 of each other and share a backbone. Their ordering is noise; the bootstrap intervals overlap almost completely. The useful statement is 'the rating backbone clears the luck test', not 'rest adjustments are the best strategy'. Ranking overlapping intervals and calling it a competition would be the exact failure mode this project is trying to avoid.

What would change the board. A licence for historical closing prices would make G9 passable, and about 200 more graded games would make G3 passable. Both are concrete and scheduled, which is why the leaderboard is a plan rather than a verdict.
