Notice & resources

A careful process supports an informed, challengeable decision. Do not serve a notice until the facts are checked.

Before communicating

A practical sequence for both units.

StepActionOfficial source
1 · Audit privatelyObtain the leases, complete rent ledgers, records of increases and notices, license status, and any relevant repair or petition records. Do not publish them.SF Rent Board · tenant petition ↗
SF Rent Board · inventory ↗
2 · Check dates and amountConfirm the lawful base and eligibility; apply the rate in effect on the effective date. Check fixed-term lease and any longer applicable notice requirements.SF Rent Board · annual increases ↗
California Civil Code § 827 ↗
3 · Serve written noticeFor an increase of 10% or less counting relevant prior increases, provide at least 30 days; for more than 10%, 90 days. The Rent Board says add 5 days if mailed. Check the statute and method of service.California Civil Code § 827 ↗
SF Rent Board · annual increases ↗
4 · Explain transparentlyShow old base, dollar and percentage increase, new base, effective date, and any banked portion. Provide a contact for questions. Do not treat this guide as a notice form.SF Rent Board · annual increases ↗
SF Rent Board · banking ↗
5 · Provide review optionsThe Rent Board can review lawful rent on a tenant petition. For independent legal advice use a qualified lawyer or tenant organization; city counseling is not a legal opinion.SF Rent Board · tenant petition ↗
SF Rent Board · referrals ↗
6 · Keep proofKeep a private copy of the notice, service record and calculation; consistently document neutral decisions.California Civil Code § 827 ↗
California Civil Rights Department ↗
