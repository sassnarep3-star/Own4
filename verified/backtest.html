Strategy research / Backtest

Backtest and luck removal

The model is graded strictly out of sample: every prediction for a game uses only games that were played before it. Nothing is fitted on the games it is scored on. The question is not whether it made money but whether anything it does could be distinguished from luck.

Reading the result honestly

The model's winner picks are worse than picking the home team every time (0.5714 vs 0.6000). Its bootstrap accuracy interval runs from 0.4000 to 0.7429 on 35 games — so wide that neither skill nor incompetence is established. The totals model does beat the naive baseline by 0.736 points of MAE with permutation p = 0.0261, but that p-value is one of two tests in this run, so Benjamini-Hochberg at q = 0.05 requires p ≤ 0.025, and it therefore fails. This is precisely the situation the gates exist to catch.
