Every correlation on this page is computed from the verified snapshot, carries a 95% confidence interval from the Fisher z transform, and is tested against a null of zero. The team table applies Benjamini-Hochberg control because ten features were tested at once.

The first row is the pace dependence that makes two teams' points move together; here it is small (r = 0.125) on 85 games, which is itself a finding: on this sample, a game's two totals are close to independent. The second row is the dependence between the two legs of a winner-plus-total same-game parlay.

Parlay dependence, priced

Leg 1: originated winner (hit rate 0.5714). Leg 2: over the originated total (hit rate 0.5143). Multiplying gives 0.2939; the observed joint hit rate is 0.2571 over 35 graded games, a gap of -0.0367. With n = 35 the interval around that gap is wide, so the number is published as a measured quantity with its uncertainty, not as a pricing rule. This is the reason gate G6 exists.

What cannot be measured, and is therefore blank

Teammate-to-teammate player correlations are blank. A prop combo's edge lives almost entirely in the correlation between the two players' outcomes (shared minutes, shared pace, shared garbage time). Measuring it needs per-game logs, which the official-source rule does not provide here. The corresponding strategies are blocked, not assumed independent — assuming independence would be the single easiest way to manufacture a fake edge on this page.
