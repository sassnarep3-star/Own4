Overview / Executive summary

Executive summary

Own4 builds its own NBA numbers from official league data, tests whether any of them carry signal after luck is removed, and publishes the scoreboard even when the answer is no. Snapshot 2026-11-05.

1. What this release is

A verified data snapshot, a transparent line-origination model, a pre-registered gate system, and a competition design that pays nothing until a strategy clears every gate. It is research infrastructure with an honest scoreboard, not a tip sheet.

2. Data

30 standings rows, 30 team-stat rows and 2 scheduled games were read from nba.com on 2026-11-05. 29 final scores were read from the official games pages for 2026-11-02 and 2026-11-04. 56 further final scores were already in the repository from 2026-11-02 and were corroborated rather than re-fetched: each sampled date holds exactly the number of games the official calendar lists, and all 30 teams' records advance exactly by their results in the newly verified games. The sample covers 70.2% of the season to date (85 of 121 games); seven October dates, 36 games, were never captured and are excluded from every calculation.

3. Originated lines

The model converts official final scores into shrunk per-team offense and defense ratings and produces a fair total, a fair spread and a win probability. For 2026-11-05 it originates ORL at PHX: originated total 225.9 published as a half-point line of 226.0, fair spread PHX −1.5. And MIA at GSW: originated total 230.0, line 230.0, fair spread GSW −3.0. Every price field is blank: no official NBA source publishes a price, and the official games page's odds toggle exposed no values to this session. No expected value, stake or return is claimed.

4. Signal research

Ten team-level correlations were measured with confidence intervals and FDR control; 5 survive at q = 0.05 — point differential (r = 0.921), points per game (r = 0.540), field-goal percentage (r = 0.506), three-point percentage (r = 0.448) and turnovers (r = -0.524, negative). Same-game pace dependence between the two teams' points is small (r = 0.125 on 85 games), which is itself a useful negative result for parlay pricing. Player-to-player prop correlations cannot be measured at all without per-game logs, so they are blank rather than assumed.

5. Luck removal

The strict walk-forward backtest grades 35 games. Winner accuracy is 0.5714 against a free home-team baseline of 0.6000 — the model loses, with a one-sided p of 0.7003. The bootstrap interval on accuracy runs [0.4000, 0.7429] and establishes nothing either way. The totals model does beat its naive baseline by 0.736 points of MAE (permutation p = 0.0261), but fails the 300-game floor and fails Benjamini-Hochberg once both strategy tests are counted. A real 55% edge would need about 620 graded games to be detected with 80% power.

6. Gates and competition

Of ten pre-registered gates, 4 pass, 5 fail and 1 is blocked for lack of a price. Zero of eight strategies qualify and zero rewards are awarded. The leaderboard is published empty on purpose: partial credit is how variance gets paid.

7. What is not known

Prices, player game logs, player availability and 36 games of this season are missing. The published +/- column does not sum to zero. The inherited game rows were not individually re-fetched. These are listed on the Flagged for review page with the page and file behind each one.

8. What would change the conclusion

In order: an official or licensed price history so that expected value is computable; a complete season of official finals to clear the sample floor; official per-game player logs to make prop work possible at all; and availability data so team lines stop being strength-only. Until then the correct output of this system is a verified dataset and a scoreboard of zeroes.
