How to quantify dependence (not yet measured)

For two binary leg outcomes, calculate the Pearson/phi correlation on aligned, same-game observations and report sample size plus confidence interval. Also report the joint probability and compare it with the product of marginal probabilities. For continuous player statistics, use Pearson for linear association and Spearman for rank/monotonic association; quantify sampling uncertainty by game-cluster bootstrap. Avoid treating repeated player-games as independent where shared game context induces dependence.

For a parlay, estimate the joint probability directly or with a validated joint model; marginal correlation alone does not determine the payout edge. Recompute conditional dependence by market, player-role context and lineup regime only with sufficient preregistered samples. The observed empirical values are blank because no qualifying records are present.

Luck versus skill: a careful operational distinction

No finite record can remove randomness. The practical objective is to test whether a signal adds repeatable predictive information beyond a contemporaneous market benchmark and simple baselines, after accounting for price, multiple comparisons and sampling error. Require probability calibration and positive out-of-sample incremental performance with uncertainty bounds; do not infer skill from a short winning streak, raw hit rate, or a single backtest.

NBA metric definitions used in proposed features are available in the official NBA Stats glossary; its FAQ explains pace and Four Factors at NBA Stats FAQ. These sources define statistics, not predictive success.
