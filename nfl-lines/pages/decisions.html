Decision register

Before any decision is made: the question, the evidence in hand, the gap in that evidence, and the specific action that would close it.

How to use this page. Every 'information we have' cell carries a link to the official source or names the measured artefact in data/derived/. Every 'information we do not have' cell is left blank in the underlying data, never estimated. Every gap has at least one suggested way to close it, naming the official source that would close it.

Q01
 
Question
Can my own lines beat the closing market line?
What we have
No. Measured over 4,055 games (2011-2026), the correlation between (own line - market line) and the actual result against the market spread is r = 0.0072, 95% CI [-0.024, 0.038], p = 0.65. Mean absolute error: own 10.56 vs market 10.01 on the spread; own 10.77 vs market 10.43 on the total.
Source: www.nfl.com/scores/2026/REG9 (official scores used to verify the archive) and data/derived/correlations.json entries CR-01 to CR-06
What we do not have
Nothing in the public data used here (final scores, rest, starter QB, venue) separates my line from the closing line. The market is better informed than this model on every measured axis.
Yes - the answer is no, on this evidence.Suggested action to close the gap
Do not fund any strategy on the current model. Re-measure only after an information source the market prices late is added (see Q02, Q03, Q05).
Q02
 
Question
Should injury and depth information be part of the model?
What we have
Yes, and it is already collected. NFL.com publishes an official injury report per game with Player / Position / Injury / Practice Status / Game Status. All 14 Week 10 games were transcribed (275 player rows) into data/official/injuries_week10_2026.json.
Source: www.nfl.com/injuries/
What we do not have
The model does not consume it. The Week 10 MIA @ IND total shows why: the market total is 38.5, the model's is 46.68 (an 8.18-point gap) and the official news on the same page says Miami placed QB Malik Willis on injured reserve with Kyle McCord in line for his first start. The model has no idea a first-time starter is playing.
No - the pipeline exists but is not wired in.Suggested action to close the gap
Add the official injury report as a model feature, starting with the single highest-value one: whether the club's most-used QB earlier in the season is the named starter. Both inputs are official (https://www.nfl.com/injuries/ and the starter names in the game archive).
Q03
 
Question
Can we identify 2nd, 3rd and 4th string replacements for injured starters?
What we have
No. The official injury report gives position and status but never a depth-chart rank.
Source: www.nfl.com/injuries/
What we do not have
Depth rank. Without it, 'a 3rd-string cornerback is replacing a starter' cannot be distinguished from 'a starter is questionable but practising fully'. Every depth-rank cell on the site is blank by design.
No.Suggested action to close the gap
Source an official depth chart. NFL.com team pages are the natural place to look; this session did not find an official, machine-readable depth-chart endpoint, so the requirement is logged as an open acquisition item rather than filled with a guess.
Q04
 
Question
Does the market misprice same-game parlays through correlation?
What we have
Measured correlation between 'home covers' and 'over' is r = 0.0083, 95% CI [-0.023, 0.040], p = 0.60 (n = 3,912). Between 'favourite covers' and 'over' it is r = -0.0167, 95% CI [-0.048, 0.015], p = 0.30 (n = 3,908). Both include zero.
Source: data/derived/correlations.json entries CR-07 and CR-08
What we do not have
Correlations for the specific legs a sportsbook actually offers (player props, team totals, alternate lines). Those legs have no verifiable price history in any source available here.
Partially - for side+total legs the answer is that the independence assumption is close enough; for player-prop legs it is unknowable with current data.Suggested action to close the gap
Keep the empirical-joint-probability pricer (strategy C-SGP-FAV-OVER) rather than assuming independence, and extend it only when a verifiable prop price history exists.
Q05
 
Question
Are we measuring against the right price: closing line or the line we could actually bet?
What we have
Only closing lines. The archive stores one spread, one total and one moneyline per game with the prices attached.
Source: data/raw/nfldata_games.csv (third-party archive; the NFL publishes no official odds)
What we do not have
Opening lines, line movement, and the time at which each price was available. If the model's edge only exists against an opening number that is long gone, every ROI here is overstated.
No.Suggested action to close the gap
Acquire a timestamped line history. No official NFL source exists; this is a commercial data purchase decision and is logged as such rather than assumed away.
Q06
 
Question
Is any observed return skill or luck?
What we have
Measured, not asserted. Each strategy carries a 20,000-trial no-skill simulation at its own prices, giving a 95% ROI band. The best strategy by ROI, S-WIND-UNDER, returned +7.77% over 303 bets against a no-skill band of [-11.08%, +10.82%] - inside the band, p = 0.17, and p = 0.44 after Benjamini-Hochberg correction across all 15 strategies.
Source: data/derived/strategy_results.json
What we do not have
Sample size. At these win rates, detecting a 3-point edge at -110 with 80% power needs thousands of bets; no strategy here comes close.
Yes - on this evidence, no strategy has demonstrated skill.Suggested action to close the gap
Hold rewards until a strategy clears the gates. Report the required sample size alongside every result so the gap is explicit.
Q07
 
Question
How should multiple strategies be judged at once?
What we have
A policy exists and is applied: Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction across all strategies at q = 0.05, plus a pre-declared confirmation period (2022-2026) that was never used for design.
Source: data/model_params.json and data/derived/strategy_results.json
What we do not have
Nothing material. This is the one area where the process is already complete.
Yes.Suggested action to close the gap
Keep the policy and apply it to any future strategy before it is added to the leaderboard.
Q08
 
Question
Is there an official NFL source for betting lines?
What we have
No. The NFL does not publish official odds, spreads or totals. Everything on this site labelled 'market' comes from a third-party archive and is labelled as such everywhere it appears.
Source: www.nfl.com/ (official league site - no odds published)
What we do not have
An official price source. It does not exist.
Yes - the gap is permanent and must be disclosed, not filled.Suggested action to close the gap
Keep the third-party label on every market number. Never present a market line as verified fact.
Q09
 
Question
Is the historical archive trustworthy?
What we have
Partly verified. Three complete 2026 weeks (1, 8 and 9 - 45 games) were re-read directly from NFL.com score pages and compared game by game: 45 of 45 final scores match exactly. Seven structural integrity checks also pass, including a full recomputation of W/L/T/PF/PA for all 32 teams from the game log against the official standings table.
Source: www.nfl.com/scores/2026/REG1 , www.nfl.com/scores/2026/REG8 , www.nfl.com/scores/2026/REG9 , www.nfl.com/standings/league/2026/REG
What we do not have
Weeks outside 1, 8 and 9 of 2026, and every season before 2026, were not re-read page by page. Week 5 of 2026 could not be checked at all: the official page rendered every score as the literal text 'Hidden Score'.
Partially.Suggested action to close the gap
Extend the spot-check to one week per season before any result is treated as settled, and record the 'Hidden Score' rendering as an irregularity (IR-01).
Q10
 
Question
What should be staked, and by whom?
What we have
Nothing is staked. Every figure on this site is in paper units at 1 unit per bet, and no money has been committed.
Source: data/model_params.json (stake_units = 1.0)
What we do not have
Bankroll size, risk tolerance, Kelly vs flat staking, and who authorises a real bet. None of these has been supplied.
No.Suggested action to close the gap
Supply a bankroll policy before any strategy leaves the lab. Until then the leaderboard scores skill, not money.
Q11
 
Question
Should playoff and international games be modelled the same way?
What we have
The evaluation universe is regular-season games only (game_type = REG). Neutral-site games are handled: the home-field term is switched off when the location field is not 'Home'.
Source: data/derived/walkforward_forecasts.json
What we do not have
Playoff behaviour is unmeasured. International kickoff times and travel are unmodelled.
Partially.Suggested action to close the gap
Either exclude playoffs explicitly (current behaviour) or model them separately. Do not silently mix them.
Q12
 
Question
Is the rest / bye-week term in the model too strong?
What we have
The fitted coefficient is 0.71 points of home margin per day of rest differential, applied linearly. For Week 10 PIT @ CIN, Pittsburgh's bye (14 days vs 7) produces a 4.9-point swing, which is what flips the model's line 7.06 points away from the market.
Source: data/derived/own_lines_week10.json and www.nfl.com/schedules/2026/REG10
What we do not have
A defensible functional form. The linear term is a pre-registered simplification, not an established finding, and the bye case is exactly where a linear term is least credible.
No - flagged, not resolved.Suggested action to close the gap
Re-specify rest as a categorical feature (short week / normal / bye) and re-register the parameters before the next run. Do not adjust the coefficient to make this week's line look better.
Q13
 
Question
Which data rights apply to the archive?
What we have
The archive is a third-party GitHub repository, pinned to a commit and recorded with its download URL in data/provenance.json. It is used for research in a public repository.
Source: github.com/nflverse/nfldata
What we do not have
A licence review. The repository's own terms were not read line by line in this session.
No.Suggested action to close the gap
Read the repository's licence and data terms before any commercial use. Logged as irregularity IR-05.
Q14
 
Question
Does the model know the weather?
What we have
Wind, temperature, roof and surface are in the archive, and one strategy uses wind (>= 15 mph outdoors, unders: +7.77% over 303 bets, inside the no-skill band).
Source: data/raw/nfldata_games.csv and data/derived/strategy_results.json
What we do not have
The weather values are not verified against an official or meteorological source, so a strategy built on them rests on unverified inputs.
No.Suggested action to close the gap
Either verify the archive's weather fields against a primary source or mark every weather-derived figure unverified. Currently marked unverified.
Q15
 
Question
What is the leaderboard actually rewarding?
What we have
A z-score of the observed win rate against the break-even rate implied by the prices actually taken - not raw ROI, which would simply rank luck. Zero strategies are currently reward-eligible because none clears all eight gates.
Source: data/derived/leaderboard.json
What we do not have
The reward itself. No prize, stake or payout has been defined or funded.
Partially - the ranking rule is settled; the reward is not.Suggested action to close the gap
Define the reward before the first eligible strategy appears, so the incentive cannot be retro-fitted to whoever happens to be winning.
Q16
 
Question
Can this dashboard tell whether a current-week line is already stale or past kickoff?
What we have
The frozen snapshot says it was retrieved at 2026-11-12T00:00:00Z. On 2026-11-13, NFL.com marked Washington–New York 24–21, Final, while the own-lines output still included that matchup as an upcoming forecast.
Source: www.nfl.com/scores/2026/REG10 and www.nfl.com/standings/league/2026/REG
What we do not have
There is no live refresh, per-game status invalidation, or visible stale-snapshot warning in the data pipeline. The weekly snapshot can therefore continue displaying a line after kickoff or after a result is final.
Yes - the observed stale-line case is verified; refresh and invalidation are proposed, not implemented.Suggested action to close the gap
Before publishing or exposing any line as actionable, compare it with the current official schedule/scores, exclude games at or beyond kickoff and those marked final, display source-retrieval and model-as-of timestamps, and fail closed when the official game state cannot be verified.
