2. What the numbers actually say

The model does not beat the market. On 104 walk-forward games the moneyline Brier is 0.2525 against a vig-free market of 0.2147. The paired week-cluster 95% interval on the difference is [-0.07532, -0.00660]. This is a clean negative result, published first rather than last.

Two total estimators were pre-registered with deliberately different functional forms so the comparison would be a real test. They land within 0.7 points of each other (MAE 11.311 vs 12.012, paired t = -1.757) and both stay published: at this sample the gap is not decisive, and collapsing to one number would hide that.

Our fair spread sits on average 0.995 points from the closing line (mean absolute residual 4.553, n=104). A residual that is close to zero on average with a wide spread is the signature of noise, not of an edge — and that is exactly what is published.

Why publish a negative result instead of tuning until it looks good? Because the tuning is the failure mode. The pre-registration register fixes the rules before the data sees them, the multiplicity adjustment counts how many things were tried, and the luck panel states how much of the observed hit rate is chance. If the honest answer is "not yet", that is the answer the site gives.

4. The decision that is available today

One decision genuinely blocks progress, and it is not a modelling decision: where do prices come from? Every ROI, CLV and staking claim on this site is hypothetical until that is answered. The second is the results feed: turning the third-party archive into officially verified rows is mechanical but needs 136 page reads. Both are in the suggestions register with options and a blank verdict.
