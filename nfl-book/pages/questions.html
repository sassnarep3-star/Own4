Questions, and what would answer them

A decision can only be made reasonably when the question behind it is written down, the information in hand is listed, the gap is named, and the evidence that would close the gap is named too. Every row below does exactly that. Rows marked answered are closed with the number and the source; rows marked open are waiting for evidence, not for opinions.

Q4 — Which strategies have skill once luck is removed?

FieldContent
What we haveEight pre-registered strategies, each evaluated out of sample with an exact binomial test, a Benjamini-Hochberg correction across all eight, a seeded bootstrap interval and a relabelling placebo.
What is missingNothing for the question as asked; a larger sample would only sharpen the answer.
What would answer itMore decisions. The gate floor is 100 decisions and the largest strategy holds fewer.
AnswerNone. No strategy clears break-even with a p-value below 0.05, none survives the false-discovery correction, and the placebo shows that a random relabelling reaches the observed hit rate. 0 of 8000 possible points are awarded.

Q5 — Can a lucky staking run hide a bad signal?

FieldContent
What we haveA signal board (skill only) and a staking board (money only), plus a masking check that compares the two rankings for every strategy.
What is missingNothing; the check runs on every rebuild.
What would answer itIt is answered by the masking table.
AnswerThe two boards are kept apart and a strategy may only be staked when every signal gate passes. The masking table lists, for each strategy, whether its staking rank is better than its signal rank while its signal gates fail; such a strategy is treated as noise and is not staked.

Q16 — Which way round is a published spread line?

FieldContent
What we haveThe third-party archive's spread line, read against 136 official results: as an expected home margin it has a mean error of 9.485 points and agrees with the outcome's direction 69.1% of the time; read the other way it has a mean error of 13.206 points and agrees 31.6% of the time.
What is missingA written statement of the convention from the archive, and a written statement from each peer namespace that publishes its own spread line. Four of the six peer publications do not say which way round their line is written.
What would answer itOne line in each publication's own metadata, plus the upstream data dictionary.
AnswerIt is resolved for the archive by the check above, and the model publishes its line in the same convention. The peer publications that do not state their convention are NOT settled: their spread rows are published with the result blank and the reason attached.
