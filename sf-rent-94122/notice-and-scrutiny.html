1. Five-Step Lawful Notice & Service Protocol

Every rent increase in San Francisco must satisfy both local Rent Ordinance licensing/cap rules and California Civil Code § 827 / CCP § 1013 service rules.

Code § 1942.5(a)) or retaliatory motive (§ 1942.5(d)).Documenting objective contract and habitability verification prior to notice creation reduces avoidable procedural defects.CA-CIV-1942-5 ↗
Step 2 · Confirm Active SF Rent Increase License Before Service & Effective DateLog into https://portal.sfrb.org, complete the current cycle's Housing Inventory filing for each unit, and retain timestamped confirmation of the active Rent Increase License BEFORE serving notice and BEFORE the effective date (also verify $59.00/unit Rent Board Fee payment).Without an active Rent Increase License on the effective date, an annual or banked increase on a covered SF unit cannot lawfully take effect.SF-INVENTORY ↗

Code § 37.3(a)(1), no cost-increase excuse is required for the published annual CPI adjustment. Never assert unpetitioned repair/tax costs to exceed 1.6%.SF-INC-GUIDE ↗

Code § 827(b)(1)(A)) OR by first-class U.S. mail within California adding +5 calendar days (CCP § 1013(a)): minimum 30 days personal / 35 days CA mail if ≤10%; 90 days personal / 95 days CA mail if >10%. Email/text alone is invalid.Archive a signed Proof of Service, USPS certificate of mailing, and exact copy of the served notice in the unit record.CA-CCP-1013 ↗

3. Raising One Unit vs. Both Units — Fair Housing & Anti-Discrimination Analysis

Core Rule: Raising one unit vs. both units is lawful when—and only when—governed by an objective, neutral, documented criterion (such as an active fixed-term lease bar on Unit 1 vs. month-to-month eligibility on Unit 2) rather than arbitrary or protected-class selection.

Building Scenario (Unit 1 vs. Unit 2) Permissible / Recommended Action Fair Housing (FHA / FEHA / SF § 37.10B) & Retaliation Analysis Official Source
Scenario 1 — Unit 1's April 1, 2026 Agreement Is an Active 12-Month Fixed-Term Lease (through March 31, 2027), while Unit 2 Is Eligible Month-to-MonthPermissible & Contractually Required to Defer Unit 1 Until Fixed Term EndsUnder Cal. Civ. Code § 827(a), Unit 1 cannot be unilaterally increased during an active fixed-term lease. Applying a neutral building-wide policy—adjusting each unit at its first lawful month-to-month contract/anniversary eligibility date using the published SF Rent Board rate—bases timing strictly on objective contract status rather than protected traits (though no policy guarantees zero inquiries without case-specific counsel review).CA-CIV-827 ↗
Scenario 2 — Both Unit 1 and Unit 2 Are Independently Verified as Eligible Month-to-Month TenanciesUniform Per-Unit Formula Application Is Permissible & Advisable for ConsistencyApplying the same published 1.6% SF Rent Board formula to all eligible month-to-month units under a written, neutral schedule avoids unexplained cross-unit disparities under FHA (42 U.S.C. § 3604(b)), FEHA (Gov. Code § 12955), and SF § 37.10B, provided each unit independently clears lease, ledger, license, and § 1942.5 retaliation checks.CA-GOV-12955 ↗
Scenario 3 — Raising Only One Eligible Month-to-Month Unit While Skipping Another Similary Situated Eligible Unit Without Objective CriteriaNot Automatically Unlawful, But Increases Selective-Treatment Questioning RiskNo statute mandates raising every unit simultaneously, so deferring one unit for a documented, non-protected reason is lawful; however, unexplained selective increases in a multi-unit property make it harder to rebut disparate-treatment or harassment allegations if households differ across FEHA's 19+ protected traits.SF-3710B ↗
Scenario 4 — Raising Both Units When One Unit Has an Active Fixed Lease, Unresolved Habitability Complaint, or Ledger DefectImpermissible — Uniformity Never Cures a Contract Breach or Retaliatory NoticeRaising a second unit provides zero defense if an increase violates a fixed-term lease (Civ. Code § 827(a)) or falls within Cal. Civ. Code § 1942.5(a)/(d) anti-retaliation prohibitions. Every unit must pass compliance verification independently.CA-CIV-1942-5 ↗

4. 12-Point San Francisco Tenant-Lawyer Audit Checklist

San Francisco tenants have immediate access to free legal clinics—including Open Door Legal directly in ZIP 94122 (1722 Irving St.), the Housing Rights Committee Westside office, the SF Tenants Union, and EDC. Below are the exact 12 defects a tenant advocate checks first when a tenant brings in a rent increase notice.

AUD-01Active Rent Increase License on Effective DateChecks SF Rent Board Portal (portal.sfrb.org) to verify whether Housing Inventory reporting was completed and an active Rent Increase License existed on the effective date.Notice is unenforceable; tenant may file a Tenant Summary Petition / Unlawful Rent Increase Petition at the Rent Board for restitution.SF-INVENTORY ↗

AUD-11Cross-Unit Fair Housing & Harassment Review (Unit 1 vs. Unit 2)Tests whether any disparity between Unit 1 and Unit 2 stems from objective contract/anniversary eligibility rather than protected traits or bad-faith coercion (FHA; FEHA; SF § 37.10B).CRD/HUD administrative inquiry or civil action (with treble damages / $1,000 minimum and mandatory tenant attorney's fees under § 37.10B).SF-3710B ↗
AUD-12Prohibition on Algorithmic Pricing Devices (§ 37.10C)Verifies rent was calculated from the public SF Rent Board CPI schedule rather than prohibited non-public competitor pricing software.Civil action by tenant, City Attorney, or SF tenants' rights organization ($1,000/unit/month penalty + attorney's fees).SF-3710C-UPD ↗
