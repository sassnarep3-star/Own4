Suggestions for review

13 proposals to make the site better at decision making. Every one starts with a blank verdict: nothing is implemented until a reviewer names exactly one option. The workflow is written into the register, not into a convention.

Why a verdict column and not a to-do list. The repository already contains 26 namespaces built by sessions that each picked the obvious path. Nearly every one rediscovered the same missing price source. A blank verdict forces that choice to be made once, visibly, with its options and its risk on the same screen.

Workflow

  1. Read the proposal, its three options and the risk attached to each.
  2. Write one option into the verdict. Not “yes” — an option ID.
  3. Only then is code written, against that option, with the verdict quoted in the commit.
  4. If a later observation contradicts the verdict, reopen the row rather than silently changing the build.

The 13 proposals

P0  Choose the price source, or formally give up on the residual  S01

Problem

Three requirements (price snapshots, fair/market/difference, CLV) and every ROI number share one missing input: a market price. Today 39 of 39 snapshot rows have a fair value and 0 have a market value. Every staking figure uses an assumed -110.

Options

  • A — License a sportsbook or data-vendor feed. Closes G7 and unblocks A01-A03, C06, F09. Costs money and needs republication terms in writing. Risk: Terms may forbid republishing the numbers, which would leave the site unable to show its own residuals.
  • B — Stay price-free and rename the product. Zero legal exposure. The site publishes fair lines and skill scores only, and the market and difference columns are removed rather than left permanently blank. Risk: No CLV, no ROI, no rewards. The competition becomes a proper-score contest, which Q08 may actually prefer.
  • C — Publish a clearly-labelled non-market baseline. Publish '#our fair line vs a fixed reference' instead of vs the market, e.g. vs the previous week's published fair line. Honest, immediately computable, and it measures model stability rather than market edge. Risk: Someone will read it as an edge. The label has to be loud.

Evidence

Week 11 schedule page publishes kickoff, network and venue for 13 fixtures and no line at all. Flags FB-01/FB-02 record the consequence.

Cost · risk · recommendation

Cost: A: procurement + legal. B: a page edit. C: one day.
Risk: A is the only path to the requirements as written. B and C are honest retreats.
Recommended path: A if the budget exists; otherwise C, because C keeps a daily publishing habit alive and is the only option that produces a number every week.

P0  Make the decision-readiness table the site's front door  S02

Problem

Nineteen NFL site trees exist and a twentieth is added by every new session. The owner has no single place that says what is blocked and by how much.

Options

  • A — Decision-readiness page becomes the canonical landing page. One front door. Every other namespace becomes archive, linked but not authoritative. Risk: Feels like throwing away peer work; it is not, the work stays reachable.
  • B — Keep the current landing page and link the table from it. Lower risk to existing navigation. The table becomes one more link among 179. Risk: The problem recurs on the next session; links are what already failed.
  • C — Freeze new namespaces until the table has no unclaimed P0 row. Stops the sprawl at the cause instead of the symptom. Risk: Slows parallel work, which is this repository's actual working style.

Evidence

26 namespaces on main; the root nav generator already carries a comment about links being silently dropped by regeneration.

Cost · risk · recommendation

Cost: A: hours. B: minutes. C: governance only.
Risk: A and C together are the only combination that holds.
Recommended path: A now, then C as a standing rule.

P0  Ingest the official inactives article — it closes the biggest perceived dead end  S03

Problem

The repository treats the active list as unobtainable. It is obtainable. nfl.com publishes a dated article naming every inactive player per club, and it also carries the venue string for each game.

Options

  • A — Capture it weekly, starting now. One page per week yields ~138 named inactives, 24 club sections and 12 venue strings. It is the cheapest evidence win available. Risk: Sunday Night Football lists publish ~90 minutes before kickoff, so a single capture will always be incomplete for the last game.
  • B — Capture it twice: once mid-afternoon, once after the SNF list publishes. Complete coverage for all 12-16 Sunday clubs plus the primetime clubs. Risk: Doubles the job count for one source.
  • C — Wait until the Game Book pipeline exists. Fewer moving parts. Risk: Freezes 20 of the 78 readiness rows on an unproven PDF pipeline.

Evidence

Week 10 article read in full this session: 24 sections, 138 named players, 12 venue strings, 2 empty lists priced by timing rather than absence.

Cost · risk · recommendation

Cost: A: one parser, a few hours. B: the same parser, two cron entries.
Risk: Low. The article is the most structured availability source found so far.
Recommended path: B. Take the capture twice and store the clock time; that also turns the report into the timestamped series E05 requires.

P0  Test one Game Book PDF before promising the availability graph  S04

Problem

The requirements for 'who was expected to start, who actually did', a replacement-depth matrix and starter-level ablations all rest on knowing starters. The only official source for that is the Game Book.

Options

  • A — Parse one Game Book now and publish the result either way. A one-day experiment that either unlocks four requirements or rules them out. Risk: PDF tables are often positional rather than tagged, so a parse may need per-page coordinates.
  • B — Infer starters from the depth charts. No PDF work. Risk: Forbidden by the brief, and only 1 of 32 clubs has a verified structured chart anyway.
  • C — Infer starters from snap counts. Avoids PDFs. Risk: No official snap-count source was located, and the brief explicitly rejects inference here.

Evidence

Game centre for WAS@NYG Week 10 links 'Download Game Book (PDF)' at static.www.nfl.com. One link observed, zero parsed.

Cost · risk · recommendation

Cost: A: one day. B and C: unbounded, and disallowed.
Risk: A's downside is a day. B and C's downside is fabricated starters.
Recommended path: A, and publish the negative result if it fails. A known dead end is worth as much as a win here.

P1  Stop publishing counts that a live page can move  S05

Problem

The brief itself quotes 254 rows and 4 no-injury sections, then 279 rows and 2 sections. Read today the page has 28 sections and 347 rows and no injury-free section at all. Three different numbers, all 'verified' on the day they were written.

Options

  • A — Publish every count with its capture clock and never as a bare fact. The number becomes falsifiable rather than stale. Risk: Verbose cells.
  • B — Publish only diffs against the previous capture. Impossible to quote a stale absolute. Risk: Needs two captures, so it must wait for the archive.
  • C — Pin the count in CI so the build fails when it moves. Turns a silent drift into a red build, which is what D07 asks for. Risk: A live page will break the build on a normal Tuesday, so the pin has to be an expected-range, not an equality.

Evidence

This session's full 8-of-8-chunk read: 28 sections, 347 rows, 0 empty sections.

Cost · risk · recommendation

Cost: A: a template change. C: half a day.
Risk: Low; the failure mode of doing nothing is already visible in the brief's own numbers.
Recommended path: A now and C with a tolerance band. Skip B until the archive exists.

P1  Render 'not listed' as unknown, not available  S06

Problem

70 of 138 named inactives do not appear in the weekly injury report at all. A player absent from the report is invisible, and an invisible player currently reads as healthy.

Options

  • A — Three-state availability: available / unavailable / unknown. The report can only ever produce 'unavailable' and 'unknown'; only an active list produces 'available'. Risk: Most of the league becomes 'unknown' and the page looks empty.
  • B — Add the reserve list and treat report-absence as available only for players confirmed on an active roster. Resolves most of the unknowns. Risk: Needs an official roster and reserve feed, which is not in hand.
  • C — Leave the binary rendering and add a footnote. No work. Risk: A footnote is exactly what a model will not read. This is the defect the brief keeps flagging.

Evidence

68 inactives join to the report; 70 do not. Malik Willis (MIA) appears in no Week 10 section, which is what an injured-reserve player looks like in this source.

Cost · risk · recommendation

Cost: A: half a day. B: depends on the roster feed.
Risk: An empty-looking page is a correct page.
Recommended path: A now, B when the roster feed lands.

P1  Re-freeze the backtest corpus with a published hash  S07

Problem

Pre-registration says 136 games; the file now has 137. The registration and the artefact have drifted, which is the exact failure pre-registration exists to prevent.

Options

  • A — Re-freeze at today's 137 with a commit hash on the strategy page. Registration and artefact agree again from today. Risk: Re-freezing after seeing results is a soft form of peeking; the reason must be recorded.

Cost: Hours either way.
Risk: A is the only option that quietly permits result-chasing. Say so in the log if it is chosen.
Recommended path: C for the live shadow, B for the one published backtest.

P1  Report a p-value of exactly zero as a resolution floor  S08

Problem

0 of 500 permutation draws is published as p=0. On n=105 forecasts that is arithmetically impossible as a true probability, and it reads as certainty.

Options

  • A — Publish '< 0.002 (0/500)' and raise the draw count to 10,000 in the same run. Honest and cheap. 10,000 draws bounds p at < 0.0001. Risk: Runtime, which is trivial at this n.
  • B — Add a second, independent season before publishing any p. Solves the real problem, which is sample size, not formatting. Risk: Needs Q03's prior-season data.
  • C — Leave it; the flag already notes it. No work. Risk: A reader sees p=0 and stops reading the flag.

Evidence

backtest.json placebo reports 0/500; flag FB-16 says the same thing but the page shows the number.

Cost · risk · recommendation

Cost: A: an hour.
Risk: Low.
Recommended path: A immediately, B as the substantive fix.

P1  Make the verifier fail on a stale snapshot, not just a broken link  S09

Problem

Both existing verifiers check links and sources. Neither notices that a standings snapshot has been overtaken by nine games.

Options

  • A — Add an age field and fail the build past a threshold. A stale table cannot ship silently. Risk: Threshold choice is arbitrary and needs an owner decision.
  • B — Add a re-read check that compares three randomly chosen rows against the live page. Catches drift in content, not just in date. Risk: Makes CI depend on the live network, which is flaky.
  • C — Publish the capture clock prominently and let the reader judge. Zero engineering. Risk: This is what already exists and it did not work.

Evidence

This session found the peer standings snapshot stale on all 32 clubs; flag FB-03/FB-04 already knew rows were partial.

Cost · risk · recommendation

Cost: A: an hour. B: a day plus flakiness.
Risk: B's flakiness is a real cost; a red build for a network blip trains people to ignore red.
Recommended path: A now. B only as a scheduled job, never as a PR gate.

P2  Give every strategy a falsifier, not just a threshold  S10

Problem

A strategy currently has a rule and a threshold. Neither says what observation would retire it, so nothing can be demoted on evidence.

Options

  • A — Two mandatory columns: promote-if and retire-if, each with a number. The demotion gate gets something to fire on. Risk: Two more blanks per row until the data exists.
  • B — Derive the falsifier from the demotion gate automatically. No authoring burden. Risk: The gate is generic, so the falsifier is generic and useless for a specific strategy.
  • C — Skip it; the demotion gate covers demotion. No work. Risk: The gate covers 4 mechanical conditions and cannot express 'the thesis was wrong'.

Evidence

5 strategies carry rule + threshold; 0 carry an explicit two-sided falsifier.

Cost · risk · recommendation

Cost: A: an afternoon.
Risk: Low.
Recommended path: A.

P2  Show the model's own uncertainty on every originated line  S11

Problem

A fair line is published as a number. A reader cannot tell a 1-point estimate from a 6-point one.

Options

  • A — Publish the fair line with a stated interval and the sd used. The reader can see whether a difference is even resolvable. Risk: An interval invites false precision if the model is misspecified.
  • B — Publish only the interval width as a separate confidence column. Cheaper, less pretentious. Risk: Harder to use.
  • C — Leave it; the model card carries the sd. No work. Risk: The model card is a different page, and nobody joins pages by hand.

Evidence

model_card.json publishes sd_margin=10.454 and mu=22.959; own_lines pages publish point estimates only.

Cost · risk · recommendation

Cost: A: an afternoon.
Risk: Low.
Recommended path: A.

Problem

Decisions get made in prose inside 26 namespaces. When a requirement changes, nothing records which question it answered.

Options

Cost · risk · recommendation

Cost: A: minutes per task.
Risk: Low.
