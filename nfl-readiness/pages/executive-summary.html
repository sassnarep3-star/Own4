Executive summary

The short version: what the NFL work can support now, what it cannot, and the decisions needed first. Not betting advice.

Where things stand

  • The official record is consistent. The Week 10 report, the standings and the schedule reconcile: league points for equal points against (6,664 each); wins equal losses (144 each). 9 Week 10 games final (18 clubs), 3 live at capture, 2 not started, 4 clubs on bye (official list).
  • Injuries are captured, depth is not. 171 injury entries across 28 clubs; no club's official depth order has been verified in this session. Starters out and replacements stay blank.
  • Prices are the gate. No official price source has been identified. Without one, no ROI, edge or closing-line claim can be computed.
  • Terms are a second gate. nfl.com §1.3 restricts systematic retrieval without written consent. The hub publishes aggregates only.
  • One model is pre-registered, in shadow. Five Week 11 forecasts are issued; eight are withheld pending confirmation. Nothing is graded yet.
  • The brief's numbers do not all reproduce. The 279 row count, the Detroit blanks, and the Seattle and Cincinnati claims are not reproduced (flags F-R01 to F-R03).

What is decided

  • No strategy is ranked, rewarded or staked.
  • Row-level injury data is private; public pages carry counts only.
  • Depth is never inferred from jersey number, experience or salary.

What the owner must decide first

  • Terms: consent, licence, or manual sampling (SR-01).
  • Price policy: signal-only research, or a licence (SR-02).
  • Depth route: verify 32 official depth pages, find snap counts, or drop depth features (SR-03).
  • Canonical namespace among the 23 NFL folders (SR-05).
