## 2. Law that applies (verified from official sources)
- **SF allowable annual increase: 1.6%** for increases effective 3/1/2026–2/28/2027 (announced 2026-01-14; 60% of Bay Area CPI-U). Prior: **1.4%** (3/1/25–2/28/26), 1.7% (2024-25), 3.6% (2023-24), 2.3% (2022-23). Source: sf.gov (S1–S3).
- **Once per 12 months**; computed on base rent only; no rounding up; use the rate in effect on the effective date; 30-day written notice (≤10%), **90-day** (>10%); mail adds 5 days; Housing Inventory **rent increase license required before imposing any increase** (since 2022/2023). Source: sf.gov (S1, S4); Civ. Code §827 (S8).
- **Banking** (Rent Board R&R §4.12): skipped allowable increases (since Apr 1, 1982) add, never compound, never expire in-tenancy; a skip is banked only after ≥24 full months without any increase; notice must itemize banked years. Source: sf.gov (S4); table 571 (S3).
- **California**: Civ. Code §827 (notice), §1942.5 (retaliation; 180-day presumption), §1947.12 AB 1482 (5%+CPI, ≤10% — backstop only; stricter local cap controls), §1954.53 Costa-Hawkins (vacancy reset only on vacancy — unavailable mid-tenancy). Source: leginfo.legislature.ca.gov (S8–S10, S14).
- **Federal**: no rent control; Fair Housing Act §3604 bars discriminatory rent terms. Source: govinfo.gov (S13). Anti-discrimination in CA: FEHA Gov. Code §12955 (source of income included), Unruh Civ. Code §51. (S11–S12)

## 4. Recommendation
1. **Raise both units by 1.6%, same effective date, same 30-day process, after Housing Inventory/license filing.** Unit 1 → $4,058.92; Unit 2 → $3,200.40.
2. Hold Unit 2’s 13.1% bank in reserve; use only with full rent history + counsel sign-off. Pending (not-enacted) Sauter proposal would cap banked increases at 10%/yr.
3. **Do not raise only one unit** — not per se unlawful, but invites disparate-treatment inquiry with zero upside; uniform formula is the normal business practice and passes tenant-lawyer review.
4. If banking is ever used on Unit 2, itemize every year in the notice and keep the ledger ready for a tenant petition.
5. Verify before serving: license filed (F14), no protected activity in prior 180 days (retaliation presumption), coverage confirmed on the Property Information Map (F3), unit 2 occupants confirmed (F4).

## 5. Reasonable vs. unreasonable reasons
- **Reasonable**: annual CPI-formula increase; itemized banked increases; approved capital-improvement / O&M / utility passthroughs; Costa-Hawkins reset on actual vacancy.
- **Unreasonable/unlawful**: “market went up” mid-tenancy; surcharge for additional occupants (expressly unlawful); “I need more income” without a petition; retaliatory timing; any protected-class motivation (FEHA/Unruh/FHA); rounding up; late/improper notice; no license; waiver of rights.
