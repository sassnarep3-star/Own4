Executive summary

Current official San Francisco annual allowable increase: 1.6% for increases effective March 1, 2026 through February 28, 2027. For a straightforward annual increase, 1.6% of the stated base rents is $63.92 on $3,995 and $50.40 on $3,150. These are arithmetic illustrations, not authorization or a recommendation. Verify base rent, anniversary, any prior increases/banking, coverage, and Rent Board license before serving notice.

Because the building is reported as built in 1940, it predates June 13, 1979 and the units are stated to be rent controlled; the applicable San Francisco local regime is the primary limit, not the AB 1482 cap applicable to certain exempt-from-local-control units. A certificate, registration, unit-specific history, and legal eligibility were not supplied, so independent eligibility verification remains necessary.

Do not increase both units merely to create an appearance of neutrality, and do not single out a tenant based on protected status, complaint, or other improper reason. Apply a consistently documented, unit-specific method and have the SF Rent Board or a tenant-side lawyer review before notice. Different lease anniversaries and histories mean identical dates or rates may be legally incorrect.

Owner practice and lawyer-ready safeguards

  • Large operators commonly have centralized rent-roll systems, notice templates, legal/compliance review, and documented approval workflows; individual owners may use simpler records or property managers. These are general operational observations, not sourced claims about every company or owner; no official source establishes a single standard practice.
  • For each unit, assemble lease and amendments, all rent notices and payment ledger, dates of every increase, rent board license/Housing Inventory status, covered services, and any petitions or passthrough orders.
  • Calculate from verified base rent only; confirm the tenant-specific anniversary and eligible rate(s); issue compliant written notice; retain proof of service; do not charge before effective date.
  • Keep a consistent written policy and records explaining unit-specific differences. Equal treatment does not require simultaneous or identical percentage increases when lawful histories differ. Do not increase a unit simply as a shield against a discrimination allegation.
  • Ask a tenant-side lawyer to review the complete record, not just the percentage. SF Rent Board counseling/referral can clarify the rules, but availability and eligibility for free representation are not confirmed here.
