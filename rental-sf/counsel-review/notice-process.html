04 / Notice and process

A sound notice starts with a sound record.

A legally permissible amount can still fail if the base, timing, licensing, category breakdown, or service is wrong. Use this as a review checklist, not as a ready-to-serve legal form.

Tenant-facing language

Be clear about what the amount represents.

Communication outline only — not a statutory notice form

“The proposed monthly rent change is $[amount] ([percentage]%), effective [date]. The increase is composed of: annual allowance [rate / amount]; banked annual increases [years / total rate / amount]; and any separately authorized item [type / amount / attached City form or decision]. The calculation uses base rent of $[verified base]. Please review the attached breakdown and contact the San Francisco Rent Board or your own advisor with questions.”

Do not send this text as a substitute for a legally compliant notice. Do not fill in a banked amount until the ledger, eligibility, and City rate schedule are verified. Use any prescribed Rent Board form/order and confirm service requirements with counsel.

What not to say

Avoid unsupported explanations.

  • Do not say an increase is required because “market rent went up” unless a specific legal pathway actually permits it.
  • Do not say “insurance, taxes, or repairs” independently authorize a surcharge; use only an applicable petition/passthrough route and its substantiation.
  • Do not imply a roommate replacement itself raised the legal rent.
  • Do not describe a 2027 effective-date rate as 1.6% until the Rent Board publishes it.
