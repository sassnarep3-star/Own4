05 / Ownership and practice

Size is not a substitute for the legal test.

A company may have a standardized rent-management process and an individual owner may keep a manual ledger—but that is a plausible operational observation, not a verified fact about how these landlords behave.

Evidence limit: I did not identify an official San Francisco, California, or federal study in the sources reviewed that establishes “normal” rent-increase practices or average increase amounts by landlord size/entity type in San Francisco. This report does not claim that corporations increase more, less, or more uniformly than individual owners.

Prudent practice, not a claim about the market

A defensible owner workflow

Keep a unit-level ledger

For each tenancy, record lawful base rent, every effective increase, the notice and service proof, separate passthroughs, tenant/occupant continuity, current lease term, annual-rate source, and license status.

Banking records ↗ · License status ↗

Use a neutral policy

Apply the same checklist—not necessarily the same dollar figure—to similarly situated units. Document why each unit is eligible on its own facts; avoid decisions linked to protected status or protected tenant activity.

Substantiate exceptional paths

Do not self-create capital, O&M, comparable-rent, or passthrough charges. Identify the exact Rent Board procedure, supporting invoices/calculation, and decision or form.

Bottom line on company vs. individual owners

The key variables here are the dwelling’s legal status, tenancy history, lawful base rent, effective date, and any subsidy/contract—not a generalized stereotype about owner size. A specific AB 1482 exemption may turn on entity type, but the evidence must satisfy every statutory condition.
