06 / Tenant resources

Yes—free help is available, with important limits.

San Francisco offers free Rent Board counseling and directs tenants to community organizations. Free lawyer help is available in some settings—especially a pending eviction case—but the City does not promise free attorney representation for every ordinary rent-increase dispute.

What a tenant-side reviewer may check

Expect the arithmetic and process to be tested.

Amount and coverage

  • Is this unit subject to local rent control, AB 1482, a subsidy, or an exemption?
  • Is the stated rent the lawful base, separated from passthroughs?
  • Does the increase use the correct effective-date annual rate?
  • Were banked percentages actually available, added rather than compounded, and documented?

Timing, license, and motive

  • Was a current Rent Board license in place?
  • Was the notice delivered with the required lead time and component detail?
  • Did an occupant change get misused as a market reset?
  • Is there a fair-housing or retaliation concern based on the facts and timeline?

For a landlord preparing a review: do not rely on the tenant’s access to counseling as a substitute for complying with the law. Preserve the complete unit file, make the amount reproducible, and have counsel review any banked or petition-based increase.
