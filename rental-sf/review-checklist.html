07 / Attorney-review packet

Close the record gaps before choosing a number.

This list is designed to make a lawyer’s review faster and more reliable. Do not upload tenant names, signatures, lease scans, or payment records to a public repository or public website.

A / COVERAGE

Confirm each unit’s legal status

  • Private address and unit identifier for counsel’s confidential use; verify construction date and residential use.
  • Dwelling type: apartment, single-family home, condo, accessory unit, substantially rehabilitated unit, or other classification.
  • Prior Rent Board coverage/exemption determinations and any Costa-Hawkins issue.
  • Whether any voucher, subsidy, HAP contract, regulatory agreement, deed restriction, or other program applies.
  • Property ownership/management entity and any fact needed for a state-law exemption.
  • Current Housing Inventory submission and rent-increase license for each covered unit.

B / RENT RECORD

Rebuild the lawful base and timeline

  • Every signed lease, renewal, addendum, and written occupancy/roommate agreement.
  • Complete rent ledger from the tenancy start: every rent amount, effective date, notice, and payment record.
  • Separate base rent from utility, tax, capital, or other passthroughs.
  • Copies of every prior notice and proof of personal service or mailing, including any 2020 notice effective during the COVID rent freeze and any later reinstatement notice.
  • Current month-to-month or fixed-term status and any term/renewal limits.
  • Tenant/occupant timeline: who was an original tenant, who remains, co-tenant vs. subtenant status, and any formal termination or assignment.

C / SUPPORT

Substantiate anything beyond annual rent

  • Official annual-rate table for every relevant effective-date year.
  • For banked increases, a year-by-year calculation showing eligibility, no compounding, and the lawful base.
  • For a petition or passthrough: filed petition, Rent Board decision/certification, invoices, comparison periods, prescribed forms, hardship information if relevant, and service proof.
  • Any prior tenant petitions or Rent Board rent-history determinations.
  • Voucher/PHA approvals or program correspondence, if assistance applies.

D / NEUTRALITY

Check fair-housing and retaliation context

  • Use the same eligibility checklist for similarly situated units, while calculating each unit separately.
  • Review the chronology of repair/code complaints, accommodation requests, protected tenant activity, and decision-making.
  • Document the lawful category and arithmetic—not a tenant’s protected status or an unsupported assumption.
  • Obtain attorney review of the proposed notice and service plan before use.

Questions to resolve

Open issues for the attorney

  1. What is the lawful base rent and last actual rent-increase date for each unit?
  2. Is each unit covered by the San Francisco annual allowance, AB 1482, a more specific program/contract, or an exemption?
  3. For Unit A, does the reported April 2026 occupant/rent snapshot represent a new lease, a roster change, a rent change, or only a bookkeeping date? Does at least one original tenant remain as a co-tenant?
  4. For Unit B, were any increases imposed after June 1, 2019? If not, which annual percentages are banked and what is the first lawful effective date after valid notice?
  5. Is the current Housing Inventory license effective for each unit on the proposed rent-increase date?
  6. Which notice period, delivery method, forms, and component breakdown apply, considering the lease term and any subsidy?
  7. Does any protected activity, accommodation, protected status, or disparate treatment create a timing or fair-housing concern?

The public site intentionally omits the street address and tenant identities. Give counsel the complete, unredacted records privately; do not publish them.
