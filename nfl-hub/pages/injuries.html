Replacement starters, verified from official game books

A game book is the NFL's own post-game record of who took the first snap, who came on, and who did not dress. Comparing the same position across two consecutive weeks proves a replacement without a depth chart and without inference. Two worked examples follow; both PDFs are linked so either can be checked by hand.

Method, step by step

  1. Open the game centre on NFL.com and download the Game Book (PDF) for the week to be checked.
  2. Read the Lineups block: it gives the starting player by position and jersey number for both teams.
  3. Read the Substitutions block: entries are annotated with a depth code, for example '3QB J.Fagnano', meaning third quarterback.
  4. Read the Did Not Play and Not Active lists to separate a benching from an absence.
  5. Compare the same position against the previous week's book. Name a replacement only when both books agree on the change; leave the reason blank unless the official status report for that week names it.

What a game book proves

  • Which player took the first snap at each position, per team, per game.
  • That the player who started was below the man he replaced on the depth chart, when the replacement is annotated with a depth code such as 3QB.
  • The direction of a lineup change: inactive one week and starting the next is a promotion, not an injury replacement.

What it cannot prove

  • Why the change happened. The game book does not carry an injury designation; the reason stays blank unless the official status report for that week is also captured.
  • The depth order before kickoff. The book is published after the game, so it cannot be used to price a game that has not been played.
  • Anything about a player who was active but did not start, unless he appears in the substitution list.
  • Post-game only: usable for verifying and back-testing replacement starters, not for setting a pre-kickoff line.
  • The depth code is quoted only where the game book prints one; most substitutes carry no code, so their depth rank stays blank.
  • Names must be transcribed by hand or with a PDF text extractor; the PDFs carry an NFL copyright notice, so they are cited and summarised, never redistributed.
  • Two examples establish that the method works. They do not establish the size of the effect on results - that needs a full season of books (see suggestion S-16).

Weekly capacity: 16 game books per week (one per game), covering both teams; roughly 30 minutes to transcribe by hand, or scriptable from the PDF text layer.

The defensive backup problem — why it is still blank

Requested: know when defences are down to 2nd, 3rd or 4th-string players.

Verified blocker: the NFL.com team depth-chart URL returns "404 - Flag on the Play" (SRC-14) and no official depth publication was located. The official injury report names the player and his practice status, but not his place on the depth chart.

Partly closed: the game books above prove who actually started at each position and, in GB-1, that a player coded 3QB took the first snap the following week. That settles the post-game question — was this a third-string replacement? — but not the pre-kickoff one: who starts on Sunday?

Therefore: the starter-weighted availability index is still not computed. A number used to price a future game would imply a depth order that no official source publishes before kickoff. Offensive "starters vs backups" has the same limitation.

What would settle it: a licensed pre-kickoff depth or snap-count feed, plus an explicit definition of "starter" (see Q-05 and Q-21). For the post-game side, suggestion S-16 would transcribe all 16 weekly books into a ledger.
