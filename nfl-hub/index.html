Three decisions that block everything else

These are the choices a reviewer must make before more building is reasonable. Full option analysis: Suggestions & decisions ↗.

D1 Which NFL workspace is canonical?

Four dashboards publish different lines for the same weeks. A reader cannot tell which is current, and search engines will split authority across all four.

  • D1-a Keep nfl-hub and freeze the other four — One maintained surface; this hub reconciles to official standings and publishes its own limits. Risk: Older pages remain reachable and may contradict the hub.
  • D1-b Keep nfl-quant and extend it — It already has a pipeline and pre-registered gates. Risk: Its lines are Week 9 and its 2026 market inputs are stale; extending it would mix two methodologies.
  • D1-c Publish a comparison page and keep all five — Maximum transparency. Risk: Worst for a casual reader; the contradiction remains.

Recommendation: D1-a. Add a banner to the four older NFL pages pointing to the hub, and remove them from the root navigation. Keep them as an archive.

D2 Buy or license a real market feed, or stay archive-only?

With no timestamped market/player feed there is no way to measure closing-line value, price props, or validate parlay payouts. Every strategy is capped at 'research-only'.

  • D2-a License a commercial odds + player-prop API with historical timestamps — Unlocks CLV, prop lines, SGP pricing and honest G7. Risk: Recurring cost; contract review; redistribution limits.
  • D2-b Stay with free archives — Zero cost, fully reproducible. Risk: No 2026 lines, no props, no parlay prices; cannot validate a real edge.
  • D2-c Manual price snapshots at fixed times — Cheap. Risk: Not reproducible, not auditable, and conflicts with the no-manual-input requirement.

Recommendation: D2-a if the project is meant to produce executable lines; otherwise D2-b and accept that the output stays a research demo.

D3 What does the leaderboard actually reward?

Rewarding raw ROI rewards luck. A strategy can top a table with 40 bets and no skill.

  • D3-a Reward the lower confidence bound of net ROI after gates — Punishes small samples and variance; aligns reward with evidence. Risk: Harder to explain.
  • D3-b Reward raw ROI with a minimum sample — Simple. Risk: Still luck-dominated until n is large.
  • D3-c No rewards until G8 forward replication passes — Most conservative. Risk: Nothing to compete over for a full season.

Recommendation: D3-a, with the pool rolling over when no strategy qualifies. This is what the current leaderboard implements: 0 points awarded because no strategy passed.

How the numbers are produced

  1. Collect. Official standings, schedule and injury report are captured with timestamps. History comes from a pinned third-party archive commit.
  2. Reconcile. The archive is re-aggregated and compared with the official standings for all 32 teams before any model runs.
  3. Model. A walk-forward Elo and exponentially weighted points model produce a margin and total for each game, using only prior weeks.
  4. Pre-register. Gates and strategy rules are committed to git before the holdout is scored.
  5. Test. Holdout ROI, week-clustered bootstrap intervals, placebo permutations and Holm correction decide the tier.
  6. Publish the failures. Anything unverified is blank and flagged rather than estimated.

Limits. Lines and ratings are derived model output from a third-party archive, not official NFL data. Market lines, depth charts and player-prop values are unavailable and are left blank. Nothing here is a recommendation to wager.
