Executive summary

Date: 2026-11-06 · Prepared for the owner of a two-unit, 1940-built building in San Francisco’s Outer Sunset (ZIP 94122). Anonymized throughout; no personal information published; dates and amounts as provided.

1. What was asked

Determine the reasonable and normal rent increase for two rent-controlled units (Unit 1: $3,995, continuing tenancy since Feb 1, 2025; Unit 2: $3,150 since June 1, 2019), under local, state and federal law; explain how corporate landlords and individual owners set increases; state how to notify tenants lawfully; stress-test the plan against SF tenant-side counsel; and answer whether to raise one unit or both.

2. Key findings (all source-linked)

#FindingSource
F-1Building vintage 1940 ⇒ almost certainly rent-controlled. Units in buildings occupied before June 13, 1979 (excluding single-family homes/condos with tenancies starting on/after 1996, and narrow exceptions) are covered by SF’s Rent Ordinance — annual increase limits and just-cause eviction protection.SF.gov — SF rental laws
F-2Current allowable annual increase: 1.6% (2026-03-01 → 2027-02-28), 60% of Bay Area CPI change; 2025-26 was 1.4%; 2024-25 was 1.7%. Once every 12 months; no rounding up; base rent only; cannot be imposed without a current rent increase license (Housing Inventory report).SF.gov Rent Board (2026-01-14) · SF.gov — rent increases
F-3Historic range is small. Official list 1982→2027: min 0.1% (2010-11), max 7.0% (1982-84), median 1.9%, mean ≈2.4%. A 1.6% increase is squarely normal for SF; anything above ~4% would be unusual absent banked increases or a Board petition.SF.gov — Allowable Annual Increases (PDF)
F-4Unit 1: continuing tenancy since 2025-02-01 at $3,995 (renewed 2026-04-01 unchanged). Anniversary = Feb 1. 2026-27 rate 1.6% ⇒ +$63.92 ⇒ $4,058.92 effective Feb 1, 2027. The skipped 2025-26 1.4% (+$55.93) banks only once 12 full months elapse (i.e., Feb 2027); combining gives 3.0% ⇒ $4,114.85. 30-day (35 mailed) notice; ≤10%.units.html · SF.gov — rent increases (banking example)
F-5Unit 2: tenancy since 2019-06-01 at $3,150; no increase since. Banked percentages for 2020-06-01→2026-06-01: 1.8+0.7+2.3+3.6+1.7+1.4+1.6 = 13.1% ⇒ +$412.65 ⇒ $3,562.65 (no compounding; current base × total %). A stricter reading of the “24 full months” rule on the banked-increases page gives 10.1% through 2024-06-01 ⇒ $3,468.15. >10% ⇒ 90-day (95 mailed) notice either way. June 1, 2027 increases use the not-yet-published 2027-28 rate.units.html · SF.gov — banked increases · flag F1
F-6AB 1482 does not override. State law caps increases at 5% + CPI (SF: 8.8% for Aug 2026–Jul 2027), max 10%, but units already under a stricter local ordinance (1.6% < 8.8%) stay under the local cap. Excess-payment liability: damages + up to 3× willful; AG/city attorney may enforce. AB 1482 sunsets Jan 1, 2030.SF.gov — AB 1482 · Civ. Code §1947.12
F-7Federal law: no rent cap — only anti-discrimination and anti-collusion limits. HUD enforces the Fair Housing Act (race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, disability). U.S. DOJ sued RealPage and landlords for algorithmic price coordination (Aug 2024; amended Jan 2025) — coordinated pricing is unlawful regardless of market.HUD — Fair Housing Act · DOJ PR 24-1047
F-8No right to reset to market. Unit 1 is a continuing tenancy (an original occupant remains) — Costa-Hawkins/§6.14-style resets require the last original occupant to vacate (or a genuine subtenant analysis); April 1, 2026 occupancy change yields $0 of additional lawful rent. Adding a roommate is never a lawful basis for an increase, even if the lease says so.SF.gov — rent increases · SF.gov — §6.14 / Costa-Hawkins petitions
F-9Legitimate reasons for an increase are narrow. CPI-based annual amount; banking of skipped years; Board-approved petitions (capital-improvement, operating & maintenance, special circumstances, utility/bond passthroughs); true vacancy (market reset). Never: retaliation (Civ. Code §1942.5), discrimination (FHA/FEHA), roommate surcharge, unapproved above-cap, missing license, improper service.SF.gov — landlord petitions · Civ. Code §1942.5
F-10Raise both units, same documented method. One-unit-only increases invite tenant-side inquiry (especially where households differ in protected class); a uniform, formula-based policy — plus objective documentation for Unit 2’s lawful banked difference — is the defensible approach. See discrimination analysis.discrimination.html · CA CRD — housing discrimination

3. Recommended course (lawyer-scrutiny ready)

  1. Week 0: confirm coverage & exemption questions with the SF Rent Board counselor (415-252-4600); pull each unit’s complete rent history; verify certificate-of-occupancy and condo status; confirm the Housing Inventory report is filed and the rent increase license is current.
  2. Unit 1 — notice by Dec 1, 2026 (30 days personal / 35 mailed, conservative + buffer) for +1.6% ($63.92 → $4,058.92) effective Feb 1, 2027 — or 3.0% ($119.85 → $4,114.85) including the banked 1.4% only after counsel confirms the banking interpretation (flag F1).
  3. Unit 2 — decision by Feb 2027: choose (a) conservative annual-only (rate TBA for June 1, 2027), or (b) banked: minimum 10.1% ($318.15 → $3,468.15, unambiguous under both banking readings), up to 13.1% ($412.65 → $3,562.65) — 90-day (95 mailed) notice required either way; serve by ~March 2, 2027 for June 1, 2027. Do not add the 2027-28 annual (not published).
  4. Same method both units; same forms; same service proof; same retention. Write the policy memo before serving; keep it with the rent history.
  5. Independent review: have a landlord-side attorney review the notice package; the brief explicitly asks that an increase pass review by tenant-friendly counsel — share the same package with tenant-side counsel (e.g., via the SF Rent Board referral listing / Eviction Defense Collaborative 415-659-9184) or at minimum a mock tenant-lawyer challenge using the checklist in discrimination.html.
  6. Do not attempt a market reset, a roommate surcharge, out-of-cycle increases, or any increase while a §1942.5-sensitive complaint is within 180 days.

4. What this does NOT do

  • It is not legal advice and creates no attorney–client relationship.
  • It does not decide coverage — that requires the building’s CO date and ownership facts (flag F3).
  • It does not certify banking math — two official sf.gov pages state different banking rules (flag F1); the numbers are arithmetic on published percentages, not a legal determination.
  • It does not publish any tenant personal information; identifiers remain with the owner.
  • Amounts marked “TBA/unverified” are blank until the Rent Board publishes the 2027-28 rate.
