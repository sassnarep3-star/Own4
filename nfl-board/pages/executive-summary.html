Executive summary

The brief asks for a system that originates its own NFL lines, ranks strategies only on selectively skill-based evidence, and knows when a starter is replaced by a backup. This page states where that stands in numbers, what the single most useful next action is, and which decisions are yours.

Six sentences that carry the release

  1. The research machinery is sound and claims no edge. A walk-forward backtest over the available forecasts ranks nobody, the control baselines are published beside the strategies, one strategy is pre-registered with a hash that predates its evaluation window, and the demotion gate is armed and has correctly never fired.
  2. The league's terms prohibit how this repository collects data, and nobody had read them. Section 1.3 of the terms forbids "systematic retrieval ... to create or compile ... a database" without written consent, and the site publishes a compiled row-level injury table, reserve list and results corpus. This is not a policy preference to decide later; it is the operative constraint on what may stay public (IR-13, Q12, SG-06).
  3. No price can be official, and that is the largest single constraint on the betting half. The league publishes no lines. Every market number in the repository comes from a third-party endpoint and is labelled as such on the row — but one page banner still says "official sources only", which is the labelling defect that matters most (IR-02, SG-05).
  4. The availability data is better than the site admits. Two peer datasets say the "who actually started" side cannot be sourced. It can: the official inactives article covers both clubs in each of 12 Sunday games, and every game centre publishes a Game Book whose participation table is already parsed once with zero collisions. That single correction moves three requirement lines (SG-10).
  5. Depth is the hard problem, and it is 32 separate problems. Two clubs' charts parse, one is an image, one prints no date. A league-wide route returns 404. Until a dated, parseable chart exists per club, any backup-deficiency feature is a fabrication waiting to happen (SG-02).
  6. Several brief claims were already fixed at the source. The Lions designations, the stale Week-10 counts and Baltimore's quarterback depth are all correct on the live page today. They are listed on the evidence page rather than quietly dropped, because the fix is only trustworthy if you can see what was wrong.

What this session did not do

It did not originate a new line, fit a model, compute a correlation or run a backtest. Those numbers already exist in peer workspaces and were reviewed, not reproduced. Producing a new number would have meant either re-deriving one from data already published (noise) or inventing one (worse). The single exception is the corpus spot-check: one settled game was compared to its official page and matched exactly, which is a check, not a model.

It also did not populate the 32-club depth register or refill the availability graph, because both change files owned by other sessions. Both are proposed as SG-02 and SG-10 with the work specified.

The 5 findings that could make a published number wrong

  • IR-01 — Every price in the repository is third-party, including the 39-row 'opening and closing numbers' store that RQ-01 asks for
  • IR-05 — Four of five catalogue strategies are not pre-registered, so their numbers are not admissible on a leaderboard
  • IR-08 — The 'pre-registered backtest corpus' is not registered and carries an unauthorised price source
  • IR-10 — A point-in-time ledger with one time point cannot prevent lookahead
  • IR-13 — The public/private split is declared and not built, and the league's terms prohibit the retrieval method that built the published tables
