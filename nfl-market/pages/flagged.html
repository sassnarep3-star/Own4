MK-01highPricesThe NFL publishes no prices at all, so every price here is third-partyhttps://www.nfl.com/schedules/2026/by-week/week-10 carries kickoff time and network and no number of any kind. There is no spread, total, moneyline or prop price anywhere on nfl.com. Every price in this namespace therefore comes from one commercial provider, DraftKings, syndicated through ESPN's public endpoint, and is labelled third-party on every row.No price here can be called verified in the sense the brief means. MK-02highPrices13 of 14 week-10 games have no closing line, because they have not playedA closing line exists only once a market closes. At read time one week-10 game had finished (WAS @ NYG, 2026-11-12) and thirteen had not. The store holds both snapshots for all fourteen, but for the thirteen unplayed games the 'close' is the provider's current number, which will keep moving until kickoff.Any study described as 'against the closing line' is, for this slate, a study against a snapshot that is not a close. The column is filled because the provider fills it; the label says snapshot, not close, and this flag says the difference matters.needs reviewsource
MK-03mediumPricesThe store covers week 10 only; earlier weeks were not archivedFifteen week-9 games are listed in this namespace with no archived prices. Their open/close data exists at the provider but was not read and transcribed this round, and the rule here is that a row is only published if its value was actually read. Writing 15 plausible payloads from expectation would repeat exactly the mistake DP-05 records.Opening-to-closing movement can be shown for one slate, not as a sample. No movement rate is published as a statistic.needs reviewsource
MK-04highPricesCaught: four games were attached to the wrong prices by orderingThe first build mapped the provider's fourteen odds payloads onto the nfl.com slate by position in the list. The provider returns a different order than nfl.com prints, so four games silently received another game's prices. None of them errored: every number looked plausible. The check that caught it was IC-01, which compares each stored home spread against the provider's own 'Team -X' details string.Every price in the store is now keyed by an explicitly written event id and re-checked on each build. IC-01, IC-02, IC-03 and IC-04 all run in the pipeline and the build refuses to be described as clean unless all four pass. The near-miss is published because a raw payload that is internally consistent can still be the wrong payload.needs reviewsource

DP-01highDepthThe NFL publishes no depth chart, so '2nd string' cannot be sourcedhttps://www.nfl.com/teams/kansas-city-chiefs/depth-chart and https://www.nfl.com/depth-charts/ both return the 404 page '404 - Flag on the Play' as re-read this session. DP-02highDepthGame Book lineups are post-game, so depth cannot be priced pre-gameThe Game Book is published after the game. It is ground truth for grading who actually started, but it cannot price a market. Before kickoff the only official signal is the injury report's practice and game status: who is doubtful, questionable, out.Replacement depth is gradeable after the fact and not forecastable from official sources before the fact. This is a structural limit on any strategy that wants to price a backup, and it is published rather than worked around. It is also the single largest open question in the questions register (QM-04).needs reviewsource

DP-05highDepthCorrection: a first draft of the week 9 injury block was wrongThe first transcription of the Washington week-9 injury block in this namespace carried eleven names that are not on https://www.nfl.com/injuries/league/2026/reg9 (Austin Ekeler, Marshon Lattimore, Terry McLaurin, Jeremy McNichols, Samaje Perine, Bobby Wagner, Laremy Tunsil, Tress Way and three others).
