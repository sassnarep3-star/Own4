Suggestions, to be chosen rather than approved

Every item below is a proposal. None has been implemented. Each carries at least two real options, a recommendation with its reason, an honest effort estimate, what it depends on, and what it explicitly does not do. A suggestion that cannot say what it fails to fix is not worth reviewing.

SM-01 · Publish a source-class label on every number and make it filterable

Problem. The site mixes official values (scores, standings, injury report, game books) with third-party values (every price) in tables that look identical. A reader cannot tell which is which without opening links.

Evidence. This page: every price traces to one ESPN endpoint; every score traces to nfl.com. The store already records the class per row.

OptionWhat it doesSource
Aadd an 'official / third-party / derived' badge column to every tablesource
Bsplit each table into two, official above the rule, third-party belowsource
Cleave as is and rely on the Flagged pagesource

Recommended. A, because it is one column, it cannot be forgotten in a new table, and it is checkable by a script.

Effort. small - the data already carries the label  ·  Depends on. nothing

What it does not do. It does not make third-party numbers trustworthy; it makes them visibly third-party.

SM-02 · Freeze the grading price before the season, not after each result

Problem. Grading against open, against a lead-time snap, or against close gives three different ROIs for the same bets. If the choice is made after seeing which is kindest, the ROI is fitted.

Evidence. The store holds both snapshots for all 14 games and the 13 unplayed games' 'close' is still moving.

OptionWhat it doesSource
Agrade against the open, alwayssource
Bgrade against a fixed lead time (e.g. 60 minutes before kickoff)source
Cgrade against the close, accepting that it needs the game to startsource
Dpublish all three and rank on the worstsource

Recommended. B for decisions, with A and C published alongside for transparency. A lead-time snap is the only one that matches what a bettor could actually have taken.

Effort. small to define, medium to implement (needs a scheduled snapshot)  ·  Depends on. QM-02 / D-2

What it does not do. It does not remove the single-book problem.

SM-03 · Add a second independent price source before any edge claim

Problem. One book cannot show dispersion, and dispersion is where the retail edge is claimed to live. With one book there is no way to distinguish 'the market is wrong' from 'this book is wrong'.

Evidence. The archive holds exactly one provider for all 84 rows.

OptionWhat it doesSource
Aadd a second provider and publish the per-market dispersionsource
Bdo not add one; restrict all claims to 'relative to this book'source

Recommended. A if QM-01 is answered yes, otherwise B, stated loudly.

Effort. medium - new archive, new integrity checks, new labels  ·  Depends on. QM-01 / QM-03

What it does not do. It does not make either source official.

SM-04 · Measure the injury report instead of assuming it

Problem. The site treats 'Out' and 'Questionable' as if they mean what a reader assumes. Nobody has measured how often a Questionable player actually fails to start.

Evidence. One club-week is transcribed here: Lucas Patrick, listed Out with a concussion, was Not Active and was replaced by Nick Allegretti at centre. That is one data point, from two documents, for one club.

OptionWhat it doesSource
Arun the season-long join (game books x injury reports) and publish hit rates by designationsource
Bpublish the one worked example and stopsource

Recommended. A. It is official-only, it is bounded, and it answers the single most load-bearing assumption on the site.

Effort. large - roughly 270 game books, but fully mechanical  ·  Depends on. nothing official; does not need QM-01

What it does not do. It does not tell you who the backup is before kickoff.

SM-06 · Make the model earn its place against a naive baseline first

Problem. The site publishes an own line for 14 games before showing that the line beats 'home team by 2'. A model that cannot beat a constant is decoration.

Evidence. The prior session did exactly this test and lost to the market (10.56 vs 10.01 mean absolute error), which is the honest outcome and should be the first table on the page.

OptionWhat it doesSource
Apublish baseline vs model vs market error before any linesource
Bpublish the lines and the errors further downsource

Recommended. A, on the landing page, because the error comparison is the finding and the lines are the illustration.

Effort. small  ·  Depends on. QM-06

What it does not do. It does not make the model good; it makes its quality knowable at a glance.

SM-07 · Set the minimum n from a power calculation, not from taste

Problem. The demotion gate uses n >= 200 because it had to use something. A number chosen for convenience will be argued with the moment it demotes something someone likes.

Evidence. One prior strategy has n = 119 and would fail the floor on size alone, whatever its edge.

OptionWhat it doesSource
Achoose the smallest worthwhile edge and compute n from itsource
Bkeep 200 and describe it as a convention, not a standardsource

Recommended. A. One afternoon of arithmetic buys a standard that cannot be argued with.

Effort. small  ·  Depends on. QM-07

What it does not do. It does not resolve multiple testing, which is separate.

SM-08 · Freeze the strategy family and publish the count of discarded rules

Problem. A multiple-testing correction is only as honest as the family it corrects over. Rules tried and abandoned are part of the family and nobody counted them.

Evidence. Fifteen rules are published; the number tried is unknown. The smallest BH-corrected p-value in the leaderboard is 0.44.

OptionWhat it doesSource
Aa register of every rule ever tried, with its date, now and going forwardsource
Bcorrect only over published rules and say sosource

Recommended. A. Without it, the correction understates the search and every p-value is optimistic by an unknown amount.

Effort. small to start, needs discipline forever  ·  Depends on. QM-08

What it does not do. It cannot recover the count for rules already abandoned.

source

SM-09 · Price parlays from the observed joint, and show the independence gap

Problem. Multiplying leg prices assumes independence. For same-game legs that is known to be wrong, and the size of the error is exactly the size of the house edge a parlay quietly carries.

Evidence. The prior session's implementation already uses the empirical joint; its measured dependence for 'home covers and over' is 0.0083 on n = 3,912, which is small but is a third-party number.

OptionWhat it doesSource
Ashow, side by side, the independence-priced parlay and the joint-priced one, with the gapsource
Bshow only the joint pricesource

Recommended. A. The gap IS the finding, and it is the one number that teaches a reader why parlay pricing matters.

Effort. small, given a joint estimate  ·  Depends on. QM-09

What it does not do. It does not produce an official-sourced joint.
