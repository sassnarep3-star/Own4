Origination: side, then total, then moneyline

The brief fixes this order. The substantive reason matches it: the side is the only market where a points model has anything to say, the total is a score-level claim that is largely independent of the side, and the moneyline is a monotone transform of the same margin distribution, so publishing it first would present a derived number as an independent one.

Three columns, never one. own_line, market_open and market_close are three separate columns; difference_vs_open and difference_vs_close are two more. The difference is never folded into the market column.

Model constants are published so they cannot be moved later: home field 1.8 points, margin sigma 13.0 points. Neither is fitted to any outcome, which also means neither has been optimised — the model is a baseline being made visible, not a product.

3 · Moneyline

The moneyline is a monotone transform of the same margin distribution that produced the side, so it is published last and the pair is printed as fair prices with no margin. Comparing a fair price to a real price is not an edge calculation: the real price contains the book's margin, which this page does not attempt to estimate.
